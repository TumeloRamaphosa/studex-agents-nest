## Generated Post Content

**Most "Biltong" Isn't Biltong. Here's How to Tell.**

Biltong is air-dried, not smoked. That's the one line that separates it from jerky, from braaibroodjies, and from every "beef stick" on the shelf next to the register.

The process is slower than it needs to be. Meat is salted, seasoned with coriander and chilli, hung in a curing chamber, then dried over days until the moisture is gone and the flavour concentrates. Nothing is rushed and no oven is doing the work for it.

That slowness is the product. It's why real biltong tastes like more than dried meat, and why the cheap version tastes like cardboard.

Three things to check on any pack:
1. Is it air-dried — not smoked, not microwaved?
2. Does the ingredient list read like a butcher wrote it: meat, salt, coriander, chilli?
3. Is the meat whole-muscle, or a paste that crumbles?

Number three is the giveaway. If it crumbles like paste, it was processed hard and cheap.
