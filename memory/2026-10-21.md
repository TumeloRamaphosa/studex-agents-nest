## TOPIC 1: COFFEE — Who is buying, and where the gate is now

### Key Findings

- **China has formally opened the door.** From **20 July 2026** a unified phytosanitary system admits coffee beans from **all 53 African countries**. Separately, **0% import tariff on all 53 African countries ran from 1 May 2026 to 30 Apr 2028** (Rwanda as an LDC has had 100% zero-tariff since 1 Dec 2024). African coffee into China went **$157M (2024) → $246M (2025), +57%**.
- **The market is big enough to matter:** China imported **$837M / 181,243 MT** of coffee; Brazil, Colombia, Ethiopia, Vietnam top the list. China's coffee market is projected at ~$153B.
- **UAE is the re-export hub, not just an end market.** Total UAE coffee imports **120–130k MT, $450M+**, and the re-export trade is **overwhelmingly green (unroasted)**. **60–70% of UAE green consumption is roaster + foodservice**, not retail. Jebel Ali and **DMCC Coffee Centre (15,000t green storage + roasting)** are the physical chokepoints. UAE green export price is **+82% vs 2019**.
- **Premium is bifurcating hard.** An Ethiopian single-origin lot hit **$2,376/kg** at a Sept 2026 auction. Certified organic + Fair Trade single-origin to the EU clears **€18–22/kg**. Ethio green average **$5,370/ton (2024, +3.5%)**. The floor commodity and the top specialty are diverging — most African exports sit well below the top.
- **Europe's gate is EUDR, not price.** The 2026 export playbook for the EU is now deforestation-regulation compliance (geolocation + traceability), then certification, then price. Price is third, not first.

### Opportunities for Studex

- **Our China pricing is correctly positioned.** A1 CIF Shanghai **$4,800–5,400/MT** and A2 **$4,200–4,700/MT** land inside the real specialty band, not the commodity floor. **No price change needed.**
- **The 0% tariff window is a closing door, not an open one** — it **expires 30 Apr 2028**. For Chinese buyers who are risk-averse about committing supply, "lock in your African origin at 0% for the next 18 months" is a real closing argument we are not currently making anywhere on the page.
- **Dubai/Jebel Ali is the right hub on our China page** — it matches how green coffee physically moves into China (and onward to Iran/Africa). We are correctly placed, but we say nothing about *re-export* economics, which is where the margin lives for a trader.
- **The genuine blocker for EU buyers is documentation, not coffee.** We are selling a product we cannot currently document. EUDR geolocation data + a real certificate are worth more to a European roaster than another 2 points of cupping score.

## TOPIC 2: CAVIAR — Russian supply into SA, and the risk hidden inside it

### Key Findings

- **Our entire caviar line is Russian.** 16 mentions of "Russia" and 3 of "Moscow" on the live caviar page; the landed-cost spreadsheet models "Airfreight Moscow → SA", CITES + permit fees, farm-gate USD/kg, and our three tiers (Oscietra Imperial, Kaluga Prestige, Sevtruga Medium). Russian caviar is not a supplier we chose for one SKU — it is the spine of the product.
- **The EU already banned Russian caviar imports** (2022 sanctions package on Russian shellfish/caviar). South Africa has **no equivalent ban today** — so SA import is currently lawful. But the tail risk is compounding: the Sept 2026 "targeting Russia via third countries" framework lets tariffs of **up to 100%** be aimed at goods from countries that are top importers of Russian goods. That is a **transshipment and payment-rail risk**, not a theoretical one, and it sits on our only supply route.
- **CITES constrains the whole category, farmed or wild.** All Acipenseriformes are listed; caviar exports need CITES permits *plus* a national export quota from the source state (Caspian states set these annually). Quota volume and price are set by the source government, not the market — so a Russian supply interruption is not hedgeable by "shopping around".
- **The benchmark competitor is farmed, not wild.** **Aquatir** (Wiesbaden, DE) sells into SA through local distributors (Itali & Danish Line, R500 free-delivery threshold, top SKUs to **R8,799.99**) at 2.5–5% fat content. Aquatir also runs a **sturgeon farm in South Africa**. That means our premium-vs-farmed-price gap is exposed on both sides: Aquatir owns the farmed story *and* has local supply.
- **SA duty on prepared caviar is heavy: HS 1604.14.10 = 25% or 200c/kg**, plus VAT. And a live precedent — in Malaysia a High Court ruled **fish roe imports exempt from caviar tariffs** on classification grounds. Not an SA rule, but a signal that **roe-vs-caviar classification is worth a real question to our broker**; it is a potentially large, and currently unasked, duty question.
- **Down-trading pressure is real.** Truffle-style caviar 100g jars sell at ~**€30 (~R650/100g)** in Europe — roughly 6x cheaper than our Oscietra Imperial retail. That is the price a restaurant will switch to when a client does not specify.
- **Caviar has no checkout.** It is not in the Shopify store at all; it sells via the trade page only. We are quoting a luxury product with no purchase path.

### Opportunities for Studex

- **Sourcing diversification is the highest-value action in this entire brief.** One farm-origin (non-Russian) line — even 50/50 split — removes the sanctions tail risk from the caviar business entirely and lets us keep selling the wild-Russian premium tier as an optional, clearly-labelled SKU rather than as the spine.
- **Private-label from 5kg MOQ is our real moat.** Aquatir sells branded tins at up to R8,799. We can put a chef's-label caviar in a restaurant's own tin at trade rates. That is a different buyer (the chef, the hospitality group) at a different margin — and it is already on the page but not being pushed.
- **A R650–900/100g entry tier protects the top.** Restaurants serving caviar today without specifying an oscietra are our real volume; a service-grade tier stops the price comparison from happening at the table.
- **Correct the lead-time claim.** Our page publishes "7–14 day import lead" and "SABS/customs handled". DAFF requires a **veterinary import permit for animal/fish products, applied at least 4 weeks in advance, issued in 5–10 working days once approved.** 7–14 days is a *commercial* handling lead, not a regulatory one, and publishing it as the total is a misrepresentation waiting for a first-time buyer to be caught by it. **Fix or reword.**

### Recommended Actions

1. **Add a second origin to the landed-cost model** (`caviar-landed-cost.xlsx`) — farm-origin EU/Aquaculture line as a parallel column, with duty, freight, and CITES-status differences, so we can price both scenarios.
2. **Ask the broker two questions in writing** (and put the answers in the registry): (a) exact SA HS line + duty for our roe vs prepared-caviar presentation; (b) DAFF veterinary import permit lead time, in writing. Do not publish the answer until it comes back.
3. **Decide the Russia position explicitly** — headline line, clearly-labelled premium SKU, or private-label only. Silence is not a position; it just means the risk is invisible until it bites.
4. **Add a caviar trade collection / enquiry-to-checkout path** on the store, so a caviar quote can become an order.
5. **Do not let the Oscietra quote carry the segment.** The volume case is the 5kg private-label tier.
