## 4. 🔬 RESEARCH FINDINGS (Research Hour, 22:00 SAST)

Full brief: `research/2026-10-21.md`

### TOPIC 1 — COFFEE: China / UAE / EU demand
- **China has formally opened the door.** From **20 July 2026** a unified phytosanitary system admits coffee beans from **all 53 African countries**. Separately, **0% import tariff on all 53 African countries ran 1 May 2026 → 30 Apr 2028** (Rwanda as an LDC has been 0% since 1 Dec 2024). African coffee into China: **$157M (2024) → $246M (2025), +57%**.
- **Market size:** China imported **$837M / 181,243 MT**; Brazil, Colombia, Ethiopia, Vietnam top the list. China's coffee market projected ~$153B.
- **UAE is the re-export hub, not just an end market.** Imports **120–130k MT, $450M+**; re-export trade is overwhelmingly **green**. **60–70% of UAE green consumption is roaster + foodservice.** Jebel Ali and **DMCC Coffee Centre (15,000t green storage + roasting)** are the physical chokepoints. UAE green export price **+82% vs 2019**.
- **Premium is bifurcating hard.** Ethiopian single-origin lot hit **$2,376/kg** at a Sept 2026 auction; certified organic + Fair Trade to the EU clears **€18–22/kg**; Ethio green average **$5,370/ton (2024)**. Floor commodity and top specialty are diverging — most African exports sit well below the top.
- **Europe's gate is EUDR, not price.** 2026 EU export playbook: geolocation + traceability → certification → price. Price is third.
- ✅ **Our China pricing is correctly positioned.** A1 CIF Shanghai **$4,800–5,400/MT**, A2 **$4,200–4,700/MT** — inside the real specialty band, not the commodity floor. **No price change needed.**
- **The 0% tariff window is a closing door, not an open one.** "Lock in your African origin at 0% for the next 18 months" is a real closing argument we were not making anywhere on the page. *(Now published.)*
- **The genuine blocker for EU buyers is documentation, not coffee.** EUDR geolocation + a real certificate are worth more to a European roaster than another 2 points of cupping score.

### TOPIC 2 — CAVIAR: Russian supply into SA, and the risk hidden inside it
- ⚠️ **Our entire caviar line is Russia-sourced.** 16 mentions of "Russia" and 3 of "Moscow" on the live page; the landed-cost model runs "Airfreight Moscow → SA" + CITES fees. Russia is the spine, not one SKU.
- **The EU already banned Russian caviar imports (2022 sanctions).** SA has **no equivalent ban today** → currently lawful. But tail risk is compounding: the Sept 2026 "targeting Russia via third countries" framework allows tariffs of **up to 100%** aimed at goods from countries that are top importers of Russian goods. **Transshipment + payment-rail risk, not theoretical.**
- **CITES constrains the whole category, farmed or wild.** All Acipenseriformes listed; exports need CITES permits **plus an annual national export quota** set by the source state (Caspian states). Quota is set by a government, not the market → **a Russian supply interruption is not hedgeable by shopping around.**
- **The benchmark competitor is farmed, not wild.** **Aquatir** (Wiesbaden, DE) sells into SA via Itali & Danish Line (R500 free-delivery threshold, top SKUs **R8,799.99**), 2.5–5% fat — **and runs a sturgeon farm in South Africa.** It owns the farmed story *and* local supply.
- **SA duty on prepared caviar: HS 1604.14.10 = 25% or 200c/kg**, plus VAT. Malaysia High Court precedent held **fish roe exempt from caviar tariffs on classification** — not an SA rule, but our broker has never been asked the roe-vs-caviar question. **Ask in writing.**
- **Down-trading pressure is real.** Truffle-style caviar 100g ≈ **€30 (~R650/100g)** in Europe — roughly **6x cheaper** than our Oscietra Imperial retail. That is the price a restaurant switches to when a client doesn't specify.
- **Caviar has no checkout.** Not in Shopify at all; trade page only. We are quoting a luxury product with no purchase path.
- **Highest-value action in the whole brief: add one farm-origin (non-Russian) line** — even a 50/50 split removes the sanctions tail risk and lets the wild-Russian premium tier become an optional, clearly-labelled SKU instead of the spine.
- **Private-label from 5kg MOQ is our real moat** — a chef's-label caviar in the restaurant's own tin at trade rates. A different buyer (the chef, the hospitality group) at a different margin. Already on the page, not being pushed.
- **A R650–900/100g entry tier protects the top** — restaurants serving caviar without specifying an oscietra are our real volume; a service-grade tier stops the price comparison happening at the table.

### 💡 One-line version
- **Coffee:** the demand is real and the pricing is right — **our bottleneck is paperwork we haven't produced, not beans we don't have.**
- **Caviar:** the demand is real and the margin is real, but **the whole business runs on one sanctioned-origin supply chain with CITES quotas and a payment tail risk.**

## 8. 🧭 THE LESSON OF THE DAY

*A certification claim is a claim about a **named document**. Ours had a document (Halaal) and we over-scoped it, or had no document at all (Rainforest Alliance). Neither was a judgement call — both were provable against files already in the workspace. The reason they survived is the reason a "two copies" story keeps repeating: **the claim lived in the copy, not in a source that anything checked.***

So tonight's real work was not the panel. It was making the copy come from a source that a checker reads, and making the checker fail the build when the copy is wrong.
