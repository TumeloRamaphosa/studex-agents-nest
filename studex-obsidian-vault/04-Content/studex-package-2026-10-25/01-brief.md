## 1. CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVE

**Objective.** Convert South African hospitality and specialty-trade buyers into **enquiries**, not
retail traffic. The package exists to prove three things to a buyer who has never heard of StudEx:
(1) that we can supply a *specified* product at a *trade* term, (2) that our trade terms are
genuinely stated and not "contact us for everything", and (3) that we are honest about the parts
that are slow, uncertain or unverified. The third is the differentiator: every trade buyer has been
burned by a supplier who quotes a lead time that does not exist.

**Audience.**
- **Primary:** South African hospitality trade buyers — boutique cafés, coffee roasters, hotels and
  lodges, fine-dining restaurants, cocktail bars, and specialty retailers/resellers.
- **Secondary:** trade importers and distributors who buy for resale.
- **Explicitly not the audience:** retail end-consumers (that is `studexmeat.com`), corporate
  gifting buyers (separate campaign), and Stud-Bot / AI-agent buyers (separate product, separate
  unresolved pricing — see §7).

**Single CTA.** One action, one destination, repeated on every asset:

block on every asset; the *action* is a single one. `sales@studex-group.com` (trade / platform
enquiries) may be used in the contact block only — **not** as a competing CTA, and **never** in
place of the WhatsApp number.

## 4. LEAD-TIME TRUTH — THE CAVIAR DAFF PERMIT

**This is the most useful thing a trade buyer needs and the most likely place for this copy to
over-promise. It gets its own section for that reason.**

The mechanism, exactly as registered:

| Step | Timing | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Apply for the DAFF veterinary import permit | **at least 4 weeks before shipment** | registry §2.3 |
| Permit issue, once approved | **5–10 working days** | registry §2.3 |
| Commercial handling to South Africa | **7–14 days**, counted **from the date the DAFF veterinary import permit is issued** — *not* from order confirmation | registry §2.3 |
| First shipment, end to end | **roughly two months** | registry §2.3 |

**The trap.** "7–14 days" is the handling window *after* the permit is in hand. It is not order-to-door.
The superseded caviar page (`fa4xt0vnu67u`) published "7–14 days from order confirmation" as the
total lead time and also published a duty estimate; both were wrong and the page was retired. The
coffee B2B page (`anvkfflansa9`) republished the 7–14 days figure as a total. The warmup email
engines carried "14-21 days". **None of that may reappear.**

**Approved way to say it in copy** (use the sequence, not a single number):

> Caviar is imported under a DAFF veterinary import permit. The permit is applied for at least
> four weeks before shipment and takes five to ten working days to issue once approved. Commercial
> handling then takes seven to fourteen days — counted from the date the permit is issued, not from
> order confirmation. **Plan roughly two months end to end for a first shipment.**

**Rules for the copywriter.**
1. Never lead with "7–14 days". The lead number for a first shipment is **two months**.
2. "7–14 days" may appear **only** with the phrase "once the DAFF veterinary import permit is issued"
   or "counted from permit issue" attached to it.
3. If a repeat buyer asks about *repeat* shipments, the honest answer is that the registry does not
   publish a separate standing-permit lead time — **do not invent one**. Say the permit is applied
   for at least four weeks ahead and confirm the current position on enquiry.
4. Do not convert two months into weeks, days, or a date. "Roughly two months end to end" is the
   registered phrasing.
5. Never attach a duty figure, a duty band, or a landed-cost number to any of this.
6. Other lines carry **no** published lead time except institutional Wagyu's 5-day notice. Coffee
   lead times are "confirmed per quotation". Gifting delivery times belong to the gifting campaign
   and are out of scope here (§7).
