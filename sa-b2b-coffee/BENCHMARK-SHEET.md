## 3. Where we sit

**Against Imvelo's K450/kg ex-VAT wholesale list (the only real like-for-like
row):**

| Our line | Our price | vs Imvelo K450 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Washed | R400 | **−11%** |
| Aerobic | R380 | −16% |
| Honey | R350 | −22% |
| Natural | R270 | −40% |

**Read this carefully, because it cuts both ways.**

- We are **below** the incumbent on every line while positioned as *specialty*.
  That means **the specialty premium is not being charged.** Washed at R400
  ex VAT could carry more — but we have no evidence yet that a SA hospitality
  buyer will pay a specialty premium, and the sector's own constraint is cost
  ("the restaurant industry cannot continue to carry more costs"), so pricing
  *under* the incumbent to win the account and attach training is the right
  first move, not a mistake. Revisit after the first 5 accounts close.
- **Our cheapest wholesale line (Natural, R270/kg ex VAT = R310 incl VAT) sits
  above the visible SA retail reference points (R195–R245/kg).** ⚠️ Those
  references are **different products** — a local South African roast and an
  Italian blend — so this is *not* like-for-like and it is not evidence we are
  overpriced. But a buyer can compare the two numbers in one browser tab,
  which is the same exposure the caviar brief flagged. **Flagged, not hidden.**
  If a prospect pushes back on Natural, the answer is the origin and the roast
  date, not the price — and if that answer is not enough, the answer is the
  training and the sachet programme, which is what the tier table is for.

| SA restaurant F&B income | **−0.3% in real terms** (May 2026 vs May 2025, 2019 prices) | Fedhasa, Aug 2026 |

**Read:** the buyer is margin-squeezed, so the offer must protect **their**
margin, not ours. The argument is *"higher margin per cup, stable price per kg,
and a barista your staff can keep"* — **not** "our coffee is better." And 76%
of SA coffee drinkers still drink instant: this is a **specialty conversion
market**, not a volume market. Do not build a volume plan on volume.

## 6. Who we are actually up against

| Player | What they sell | What they structurally cannot sell |
|---|---|---|
| **Canteen** | National: vending, markets, coffee, dining — *"elevate your breakroom"* | The farm, the origin, the story, the trained barista |
| **CoffeeDesk** | B2B platform; coffee, tea, accessories | Same |
| **Dish Café** | Office coffee service | Same — **and** it is a named *partner* in our own prospects file |
| **Coffee Devices SA** | Equipment + supply | Same — **and** also a named partner |

🔑 **The incumbents sell the machine and the consumable.** They do not sell the
farm, the origin, the story, or the trained barista. Our entire premium is the
thing they structurally cannot supply — which is why **training is priced as a
line on the sheet** and why the sheet leads with the origin story, not with a
discount.

**Posture:** approach Dish Café and Coffee Devices SA as a **white-label bean
supplier to their machine clients**, not as a competing brand. They have the
machine in the breakroom; we have the origin and the trainer.

## 7. The sector's four stated pains — and our answer for each

From the Hostex 2026 sector brief, named in `research/2026-10-29.md`:

| # | What the sector says | Our answer | Is it real? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | SA imports most of its coffee — *"particularly vulnerable to global price and supply fluctuations"* | Direct trade from African origins + published tier sheet so their cost is knowable in advance | 🟢 **Yes** — and our own band is volatile, which is exactly why we publish a band and not a promise |
| 2 | *"Rising operational costs and ongoing load shedding"* | Equipment guidance + grinder/brewer setup support on the account | 🟡 **Partial** — we cannot solve load shedding, but a café that cannot brew is not buying coffee |
| 3 | *"Training and retaining skilled baristas is a major issue — the profession is often undervalued"* | **The retainer.** Priced on the sheet, not a favour | 🟢 **Yes — and this is the one nobody else sells** |
| 4 | Guests now expect single-origin, dialed espresso and alt-brew in hotels, restaurants and conference venues | Four origins on the sheet + in-room sachet programme for hotels | 🟢 **Yes** — this is our whole premium |

**Say these in their language.** "Partnering with local micro-roasters and
establishing direct-trade relationships" and "building value chains with African
coffee-producing countries (Ethiopia, Rwanda, Kenya)" are named 2026 sector
opportunities — our Rwanda A1/A2 origin story is *on their trend list*.

## 8. Bottom line for the sheet

The floor is defensible: we cost on the **top** of the green band, at the
**higher** live FX, with duty, freight, roasting and packaging all added
explicitly. The ladder is shallow because Natural does not have the margin to
discount into — the floor, not the competition, sets it. The service lines are
priced because the training retainer is the only line no competitor can match.

**What we cannot prove tonight:** the green band itself, the duty line, the
freight, the roast cost and the trainer rate. Those are listed as owner
actions in `WHOLESALE-PRICE-SHEET.md` §9, not buried.
