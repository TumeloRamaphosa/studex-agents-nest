### The headline finding

The brief said three green-coffee FOB bands were live at once. **Two of the three reconcile. Only one number is actually orphaned:**

| Band | Status | Reconciles? |
|---|---|---|
| $3,400–5,200/MT — origin portfolio table | published | ✅ Different **scope** (Uganda low, SA Bourbon high), not a competing price for the same product. Rwanda A1 at $4,200–4,800 **sits inside it**. |
| $4,200–4,800/MT (A1), $3,600–4,000/MT (A2) | published | ✅ Correctly quoted in the PROWTC offer. |
| **$6.07–6.75/kg** — the internal cost band | 🔴 UNVERIFIED | 🔴 **Not traceable to any published table.** Flagged UNVERIFIED in our own assumptions register. |

**And it sits above the UAE import ceiling.** IndexBox (Sep 2026) puts UAE green coffee imports at $2.50–6.50/kg. Our internal high of $6.75/kg is *above* it — if that band is true, we are priced out of the Gulf by construction.

### What the ambiguity costs

The model is the **same model** the wholesale price sheet uses, and it reproduces the published sheet **exactly** — 25.2% and 30.7% margin, asserted on every run as a self-test. If it ever stops reproducing them, the model has drifted and both must be re-based together.

| Scenario | FOB | Landed green | Natural 30 kg+ margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| A1 band low | $4.20/kg | R72.85/kg | 46.0% ✅ |
| A1 band high | $4.80/kg | R82.84/kg | **41.1%** ✅ |
| Internal band low | $6.07/kg | R103.97/kg | 30.7% ✅ |
| Internal band high | $6.75/kg | R115.28/kg | **25.2%** ✅ |

> **The gap is R32.44/kg landed green = R1,145 per 30 kg roasted bag = R648,800 per 20 MT container.**
>
> The 30 kg+ tier carries **41.1% margin at the published band** and only **25.2% at the internal band** — a 15.9-point difference. We are pricing the published sheet on the pessimistic number.

🔑 **This reframes the risk.** The exposure is not *"we contradict ourselves publicly"* — the published set is coherent. It is *"our internal floor is probably too high and we have been pricing to it."* Our current prices clear the floor under the **worst** case, so **nothing is at risk except opportunity.** The 30 kg+ tier could carry **R188.61/kg** and still clear a 25% floor at the published band.

## BUILD 3 — 🦴 Biltong Price Ladder

**What I built:** `biltong-ladder/` — benchmark sheet + generator + proposed ladder.
**Files:** `BENCHMARK-SHEET.md` · `inputs.mjs` · `ladder-engine.mjs` · `compute-ladder.mjs` → `LADDER.md` + `ladder.generated.json`

### The finding

**There are two biltong ladders live at once and they are wrong in opposite directions.**

| Ladder | 500g | Luxury 1kg | Gold 1kg | 7kg case | Verdict |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Shopify live store** | R450 | R650 | R1,450 | R12,500 | 🔴 500g costs **R250 more per kg** than the 1kg; the case costs **R2,350 more than 7 × 1kg** (+23.2%) |
| **Marketing page + registry §2.1** | R325 "From" | — | R1,250 | R8,400 | 🟡 Structurally correct (case beats 7 × 1kg by R350) but below the margin floor at wagyu cost |

One has the right shape and the wrong money. The other has the right money and the wrong shape.

### Why I did not just print a ladder

We have **no biltong cost model**. So the tool does not pretend. For every tier it prints the **break-even raw cost** — the raw beef price at which that tier exactly clears the 25% floor — and it reverse-engineers the raw cost each competitor's shelf price implies.

| Benchmark | Shelf price/kg | Implied raw cost at a 25% floor |
|---|---:|---:|
| Woolworths chilli 150g | R1,053.27 | R219.63/kg |
| Woolworths sliced 400g | R699.98 | R95.98/kg |
| Shoprite bulk | R129.99 | **negative — below cost at any raw price** |
| Biltong Boytjies wagyu | R549.99 | R43.00/kg |

> 🔑 **The SA market implies a raw cost in the low hundreds.** If our invoice is materially above that, the product is not an SA retail product — it is an export product (US DTC band: **R1,208–2,124/kg**). That is a strategic conclusion, not a price sheet.

The 8 SA and 4 US benchmarks all carry sources and dates (`BENCHMARK-SHEET.md`). Two raw-beef anchors came out of the SA data itself: **retail bulk silverside R130–150/kg** and "at R599/kg that is based on a raw beef price of around R200/kg".

### The proposed ladder

Standard silverside (R140/kg raw) and wagyu (R320/kg raw) both run. Every tier clears the floor, the case beats 7 × 1kg, the 500g carries at most a 15% per-kg premium, and per-kg price falls as pack size rises **within each line**.

### Second correction to the brief

The brief asked for a ladder that makes the 500g **cheaper per kg** than the 1kg. That is backwards for retail: the small pack carries the **higher** packaging cost (R19 vs R14/kg) and every SA benchmark runs the other way — Woolworths charges R880/kg for 125g against R700/kg for 400g. Making the 500g cheaper per kg than the 1kg means a buyer is **penalised for buying a kilo**.

What is enforced instead: the 500g carries no more than a 15% per-kg premium over the 1kg, and per-kg price falls as pack size rises. **The brief's real target — the Shopify inversion — is closed either way.**

### Three bugs I found in my own generator

Worth recording, because they are the exact class of error that makes a price sheet lie:

1. A **pack price compared against a per-kg cost** — inflated the 7kg case margin to 89% when it was 25%.
2. The 500g **floor compared pack-price against per-kg** — pushed the pack to R735 when it should have been R370.
3. The monotonicity check **compared across product lines** — Gold's case against Luxury's 1kg is a category error.

All three are fixed, and the generator now prints per-kg everywhere. The 4 remaining `FAIL` lines in the output are the **live ladder's** defects — they are supposed to fail.
