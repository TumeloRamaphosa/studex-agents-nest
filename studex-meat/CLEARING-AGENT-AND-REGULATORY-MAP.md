### 🔑 The answer B and D give changes the lead time we've published
The authoritative DAFF service page (gov.za) states the veterinary import permit:
- is applied for by **the importer** (or an agent on their behalf) — and the payment reference
  must be the importer's name and surname;
- takes **3 to 5 working days** to issue once approved by the state veterinarian at the national
  office;
- **must be in hand before the product ships** from the exporting country;
- requires the **veterinary health certificate** to be completed in English by an authorised vet
  in the exporting country **within 10 days of departure**;
- and the **CITES permit may need to be presented at the port of entry** — alongside the original
  veterinary import permit and health certificate, to the SA veterinary import control officer.

## 2. 🔴 Our published lead time is not sourced from DAFF — and it may be overstating us

The live caviar page publishes:

> *"For a first shipment the permit must be applied for at least 4 weeks ahead and takes 5–10
> working days to issue once approved — allow roughly two months end to end."*

**gov.za says 3–5 working days to issue.** So our published figure is:
- **~4–6 weeks longer than the stated issuance time** — conservative, so not a misrepresentation
  of speed, but it is an **unsourced published number**, and the sourcing memo's own rule is that
  a number should not be published until it is sourced;
- and it may be **costing us deals** — if issuance really is 3–5 working days, our true
  end-to-end is closer to **5–6 weeks, not two months**, and "two months" is a self-inflicted
  handicap in a market where the competitor (Aquatir) is farmed and local.

**Do not change the published figure until DAFF answers.** But do ask — the upside is a shorter,
more competitive lead time.

🔑 **That implies a second CITES document — a South African import permit — which is not in our

## 4. How a clearing agent is actually appointed (SARS)

- Any agent lodging a Customs Clearance Declaration for reward **must be licensed under Section
  64B of the Customs Act** — so licence can be checked.
- **A clearing agent must lodge a bond** — the amount is defined during the application process.
- Licence runs **1 January to 31 December** each year (Schedule 8, item 835.00).
- SARS publishes a **"Clearing Agent Pro forma agreement"** — 🔑 there is a ready-made appointment
  mechanism; we do not have to negotiate one from scratch.
- 🔑 **We also need our own importer registration and security position.** As importer of record we
  are the party on the import permit, the payment reference and the CCD. **If we are not
  registered as an importer with SARS, we cannot appoint an agent to clear on our behalf.** That
  is question **A**, and it may be the real blocker behind the pricing question.

## 6. 🔴 DFFE's website serves an incomplete certificate chain — no strict client can reach it

Verified 2026-10-24 from this host:

- `http://www.dffe.gov.za/fim_rightspermit_importsexports` → **301** redirect to HTTPS
- `https://www.dffe.gov.za/` → **fails**: `SSL certificate problem: unable to get local issuer
  certificate` (curl error 60); openssl: `verify error:num=21: unable to verify the first
  certificate`
- The server presents the leaf certificate `CN=www.dffe.gov.za` issued by **Sectigo Public Server
  Authentication CA DV R36** but **does not send the intermediate certificate**. The TLS handshake
  completes; only chain verification fails.
- Controls: `gov.za` → **200**, `sars.gov.za` → **200**. `cites.org` → **403** (a Cloudflare bot
  block on our fetcher — a problem for *us*, not for humans).

🔑 **This is a server misconfiguration in a government website, not a block on our side.** DFFE is
the body that issues the **marine aquaculture import permit**, the **CITES import permit** and
the **NDF** — the three gates on our 4–8 week clock, and the body we have never contacted.
🔑 **A broken chain refuses automated submission and many standard clients.** If their
e-permitting system sits behind the same configuration, an application may fail for a large share
of applicants. **Confirm the channel by phone before filing**, and raise the certificate defect
with them — it also explains why our retrieval failed.
