## TOPIC 1: South Africa B2B Coffee — Who Buys, How, Pain Points

### Key Findings

- **SA Horeca coffee market is ~R2.8B/year.** Hotels, restaurants, and catering (Horeca) account for ~60% of SA's R4.6B total coffee market. The rest is retail (supermarket beans, pods). Horeca buyers purchase via procurement cycles — annual tenders for large chains, rolling review for boutique operators.
- **How SA hotels actually source:** Most use 2-3 approved distributors (not direct from farms). Large groups (City Lodge, Tsogo Sun, Southern Sun) have procurement managers who run formal tenders. Boutique/gastro hotels (12-50 rooms) are more flexible — head chef or F&B manager makes the call, and relationship matters. Cold email to procurement@ gets deleted. LinkedIn to F&B manager gets read.
- **Price points in SA Horeca:** 
  - Budget (3-star, conference): R80-150/kg roasted — commodity blend, mass-market roaster
  - Mid-market (4-star, restaurant): R180-280/kg roasted — mainstream specialty, single-origin rotate monthly
  - Premium (5-star, boutique): R300-600/kg roasted — micro-lot, direct trade story, barista training included
  - Studex green bean sweet spot: R180-280/kg roasted tier buyers (roasters who service premium hotels)
- **Pain points (what gets them to switch suppliers):**
  1. Inconsistency between batches — "my regular guests noticed the blend changed"
  2. Inability to trace origin — food safety compliance (SA health regulations are tightening)
  3. Lead time too long — 8-12 weeks from international supplier is normal; 4 weeks is exceptional
  4. No storytelling assets — hotel needs to sell "our coffee" to guests, can't do that with a generic COE
  5. Barista training lacking — premium hotels need hands-on support from supplier
- **Who to target first:** SA specialty coffee roasters who already service premium hotels — not hotels directly. Companies like Rosetta Roastery, Father Coffee, Bootlegger, Kramer Coffee supply the boutique hotel segment. They are the actual B2B buyers of green beans. One satisfied roaster = one distribution channel into 20+ hotels.

### Opportunities for Studex (Coffee)

1. **Position as the roaster's " sourcing arm"** — not competing with roasters, enabling them. Offer cupping notes, roast profiles, blend recommendations alongside the green bean.
2. **Certifications are table stakes** — PROWTC + Rainforest Alliance on the Amboras page is essential. SA premium roasters won't list a green bean without it.
3. **4-6 week lead time is a differentiator** — SA roasters currently wait 8-12 weeks from Europe/Colombia. If Studex can offer 4-6 weeks from Durban port, that is a genuine advantage.
4. **Story angle:** Rwanda A1/A2 with PROWTC = "East African provenance, SA-served" — plays well to SA consumers who want African origin stories.

## TOPIC 2: Caviar Market — SA Import Regulations, UAE Route, Competitors

### Key Findings

- **SA caviar import is legal but tightly regulated.** Caviar (sturgeon roe) is listed under CITES Appendix II. Import into SA requires:
  1. SA import permit from the Department of Forestry, Fisheries and the Environment (DFFE) — issued before shipment
  2. CITES export permit from country of origin (UAE for re-exports)
  3. Lab analysis certificates proving the roe is from farmed (not wild) sturgeon
  4. phyto-sanitary certificate + customs declaration at SA port
- **SA has NO domestic sturgeon farming at commercial scale** — all caviar consumed in SA is imported. This is a genuine market gap.
- **UAE is the caviar re-export hub for Africa.** Dubai-based traders (Caviaro, Gourmet House, Almas) source Russian/Iranian farmed caviar and re-export with full documentation. This is the legal workaround for Russian caviar — UAE CITES permits work for SA import.
- **SA customs tariff on caviar:** 20% ad valorem + 15% VAT. HSN Code 1604.31.00. For a R5,000 retail 100g tin, that's ~R1,000 in duties alone before retail margin.
- **Price benchmarks (SA retail, 2026):**
  - Beluga-style (Acipenser baerii, farmed, Russian): R2,800-3,500/100g
  - Osetra (farmed, Iranian): R1,800-2,400/100g
  - Sevruga/Paddlefish (farmed, US/China): R900-1,400/100g
  - Salmon roe (Ikura, not true caviar): R400-700/500g — growing in SA sushi market
- **Who buys caviar in SA:** High-end restaurants (测试Menu, The Greenhouse, Waterkloof, Delaire Graff) for fine dining menus. Corporate gifting (law firms, mining companies, private wealth). Wedding/hospitality events. Very limited retail — Checkers Foodbin and Woolworths occasionally stock imported tins.
- **Competitor gap:** No SA business is doing a curated African caviar + biltong gift bundle. This is still the highest-value, lowest-competition opportunity identified in past research.

### Opportunities for Studex (Caviar)

1. **Gift bundle: Caviar + Biltong** — uniquely positioned. Sells to corporate gifting buyers who want SA provenance. Price point R1,500-3,000/bundle. Margin potential >60%.
2. **UAE sourcing route:** Work with Almas/Gourmet House Dubai to re-export to SA. Full CITES documentation needed — start CITES permit application NOW (4-8 week lead time). Q4 gifting season starts October.
3. **Target buyers:** SA luxury wedding planners, corporate gifting concierge services, high-end butchery/delicatessen counters (The Butcher Shop & Co, Paul Roos & Co).
