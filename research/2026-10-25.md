## 🔴 LEAD FINDING OF THE NIGHT

**Our China outreach copy is factually wrong on the single most important commercial fact in the file, and it is the kind of wrong a Chinese buyer will catch.**

Our China sheet (Email Draft 1) says:

> *"a rare market opens July 20 — South African green coffee gains access to the Chinese market under the SA-China bilateral agricultural protocol."*

**Three errors in one sentence:**

1. **The date is wrong.** China's zero-tariff treatment for the 53 African countries with diplomatic ties began **1 May 2026**, not 20 July. 20 July 2026 is the date GAC published the **coffee-bean import protocol** — a separate, narrower instrument. By the time we send this email, the "window" has been open for **four months**.

South Africa (non-LDC) has a **2-year window closing 30 April 2028**. We are sourcing from Rwanda, so the *stronger, permanent* rule is the one that governs our lots. We are currently quoting our own weaker position.

**Why this matters commercially:** African coffee exports to China **surged 145.7% YoY in Jan–May 2026**. China bought $246M of African coffee in 2025 (up from $157M in 2024). Demand grows >30%/yr. We are sending our buyers a **July 20 "window opens" line three months after the window opened**, on an instrument that does not exist, while our actual supply country enjoys a permanent tariff advantage we fail to mention. Every day we send that copy, we hand the negotiation to the competitor who read the actual policy.

## TOPIC 1 — COFFEE EXPORT: WHERE THE MONEY AND THE GATES ARE

### Key findings

**1. The Chinese tariff position — the good news, stated properly**
- Zero-tariff on **100% of tariff lines** from all 53 African countries with diplomatic ties, effective **1 May 2026**. Coffee (green and roasted) was previously carrying **8–15%** duty.
- **LDC origins (Ethiopia, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, …): no expiry.** **Non-LDC (South Africa, Kenya, Nigeria, Egypt): window closes 30 April 2028.** Our Rwandan lots sit in the permanent category. *We never say this.*
- The differential is not theoretical: at $6.75/kg, an 8–15% tariff is **$0.54–$1.01/kg** handed to the buyer. On a 60kg bag that is **$32–$61** — enough to flip a comparison against an Ethiopian or Brazilian lot.
- **African coffee exports to China +145.7% YoY (Jan–May 2026).** Chinese demand growing >30%/yr. Top origins: Ethiopia, Kenya, **Rwanda**, Uganda.

**2. The value-add uplift — this is the money we are leaving on the table**
- Green FOB baseline ≈ **$3.80/kg**. **Roasted and packaged at origin ≈ $8.50/kg** — a **+124% uplift (+$4.70/kg)** — and *both* enter duty-free. Processing in Africa keeps the value in Africa and raises the ticket.
- Our current sheet is 100% green, FOB Kigali, 60kg bags, at a **$6.07–6.75/kg band**. We are capturing the *origin* premium but **none of the processing premium**. A roasted-origin offer at $8.50/kg is roughly the same customer conversation at 2.25× the revenue per kilo.
- Same logic applies to the UAE re-export channel — Dubai roasts and re-exports; a roasted product may suit that route better than a green one.

**3. What China actually requires on paper (and where the tariff gets clawed back)**
- **Phytosanitary certificate** issued by the national plant protection organisation, **in Chinese-translated format**. **Health certificate** for processed foods. **Certificate of Origin** that *specifically references China's zero-tariff preference framework* (not a generic FTA form). Commercial invoice with **HS code (China 10-digit, 6-digit minimum)** and FOB value. Bill of lading. Packing list with container numbers and marks.
- 🔴 **The single most common way to lose the zero-tariff:** a Certificate of Origin that does not explicitly cite the zero-tariff preference. Result — **duty denied, MFN applied at 8–30%**, retroactively. A document error costs us 8–15% of the contract value.
- **Buyer-side risk flagged in the trade guides:** a large share of "interested" Chinese buyers are small trading desks with a WeChat presence and few employees. **Recommendation from the sources: confirmed LC terms for initial transactions, not open account.** Our outreach currently invites a call and a sample — no payment structure mentioned at all.

**4. The UAE — a genuine second channel we have barely used**
- UAE coffee imports exceeded **120,000 tonnes in 2025, valued USD 450–500M**, with **40,000–50,000 tonnes re-exported** — Dubai is a re-export hub, not just a consumer.
- The UAE green import price band is **USD 2.50–6.50/kg** depending on grade, origin and certification. **Our $6.07–6.75 band sits at or slightly above the top of that range** — we are priced for the specialty end of the UAE market and must say so.
- Infrastructure exists: the **DMCC Coffee Centre** is an explicit trade-facilitation hub; **78 green coffee importers** are registered in the UAE; **World of Coffee Dubai 2026** (5th edition) drew **17,000+ visitors and 450 exhibitors**.
- 🔴 **We have a `uae-store` folder in the workspace and no UAE buyer list in any outreach file.** The channel is instrumented and unused.

**5. Europe — the deadline that will close the door on unprepared lots**
- **EUDR** covers coffee. Large and medium operators must comply from **30 December 2026**; micro and small operators from **30 June 2027**. Compliance requires **geolocation of the plot of production** and a **due-diligence statement**.
- ⚠️ **Our lots come from Rwanda via a washing station. We hold no geolocation data.** If our Kigali partner is not already EUDR-ready, every EU-facing quote is dead on 30 Dec — and our **China sheet has no EUDR mention at all**, even though we have an EU-position file (`foodexpoconnect` pricing benchmarks include EU-ready origins).
- EU reference pricing, July 2026: Ethiopia Yirgacheffe Organic **$4.20/lb**, Kenya AA **$4.80/lb**, Rwanda fully washed **$5.20/lb** ($5.50–6.50/lb EU range). **Rwanda already commands the top of the African field in the EU.**

**6. Price tape — the market is tightening even as it sits below last year's peak**
- ICE Arabica C **$3.06/lb ($6.75/kg)** on 23 Oct — **+11.66% on the month**, +14.7% in four weeks, but **−23% YoY**. Our live desk reproduced this exactly.
- **Arabica exchange stocks are down 43.53% year-to-date.** A declining stock against a rising 4-week price is the classic setup for a further move.
- Production forecast 2026/27 is **189.7M bags — 10.8M higher**, on record Brazil, Ethiopia, Uganda output. Supply is coming, which caps the upside. **Both directions are live; this is why the quote band is a band.**

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Rewrite the tariff story — it is free money and we are giving it away.** Our Rwandan lots have **permanent zero-tariff access to China**, a position that expires for our competitors in South Africa, Kenya and Nigeria on 30 April 2028. That single sentence belongs at the top of the China email and on the CIIE landing page. It is a *permanent structural advantage*, not a promotional window.
2. **Add a roasted-at-origin line.** $8.50/kg vs $3.80/kg green, both duty-free, +124% uplift. ## TOPIC 2 — CAVIAR INTO SOUTH AFRICA: THE PERMIT STACK

### Key findings

**1. Every tin of sturgeon caviar in international trade carries a CITES label and travels on a CITES export permit.** Not a nicety — a legal requirement under the sturgeon/paddlefish listings. CITES maintains a **Register of Caviar Exporters**; China is on it (e.g. Frenchina Sturgeon Caviar Co., Xiangxi). Our supplier's CITES export permit and the registered code on each tin must be verified before any container moves. Any caviar without CITES documentation is a seizure.

**2. The Russia question is now a legal risk, not a pricing question.**
- The EU has **banned Russian caviar imports since April 2022**. The EU is now moving on Russian cod and broader Russian seafood restrictions (June 2026 discussions).
- The US has enacted a **tariff authority of up to 100% on goods imported from countries that are among the top importers of Russian goods** (law signed September 2026), on top of existing sanctions.
- 🔴 **Implication for our own position:** if any part of our caviar supply chain, shipping, or documentation touches Russia or a Russian-connected jurisdiction, the lot becomes unsellable in the EU and US and legally awkward in SA. Our model uses Russian-basin genetics names (Oscietra, Sevtruga) — **that is a species descriptor, not an origin claim, and must never appear in our marketing copy as provenance.** The species names are fine; "Russian" as a source claim is now a liability.
- **The safe and defensible position is farmed Chinese sturgeon (oscietra/severugga) with CITES documentation and full traceability.** 96% of global caviar consumption is farm-raised, and China is the largest farmed producer.

**3. South African import permits — four separate authorities, and the stack has never been assembled.**
- **DFFE** (Department of Forestry, Fisheries and the Environment) import permit for fish products. Note the published condition: **the import permit excludes freshwater fish** — our caviar is marine sturgeon, which is in scope, but the exclusion must be checked against the specific permit conditions we hold.
- **ITAC** import permit notification required for the import of **frozen fish and fish products**.
- **DAFF** (Department of Agriculture, Land Reform and Rural Development) — food and agricultural import regulations and standards, importer registration and product-specific import requirements for food items.
- **SARS Customs** — importer registration and importer's code; a current **Schedule 1 Part 1 Customs & Excise Tariff (16 Oct 2026)** governs the duty line.
- **NRCS** — the South African regulatory body for compulsory specifications on imported food, plus **labelling** compliance.
- ⚠️ **The duty rate on HS 1604.14.10 (prepared or preserved caviar) is not confirmed in our files.** Public aggregators show the line with a 15% VAT reference but the **duty rate itself is unverified** — our workbook uses 20% duty as modelled. **One confirmation against the SARS tariff schedule closes this.** Do not assume 20%.

**4. Demand and pricing in our market.**
- South Africa has a tracked caviar (sturgeon) import market with published import-price series — a comparable-market benchmark we are not currently using.
- Global caviar market: **USD 449–519M (2024–26)**, CAGR ~**7.7–8.0%**, forecast ~USD 812M–962M by 2032–34. ME&A region: **USD 45.1M (2025) → 89.1M (2034)**.
- Retail price benchmarks 2026: **Oscietra from €1,200/kg, Sevruga from €800/kg**; US range **$2–15/gram** ($60–450 per 30g tin); Baeri sits at the low end; Golden Oscietra ~$6,700/kg.
- **Our published trade prices convert well:** Oscietra Imperial **R34,000/kg ≈ $2,051/kg** (above the EU benchmark, far below US premium), Kaluga Prestige **R22,000 ≈ $1,327/kg**, Sevtruga Medium **R12,500 ≈ $754/kg**. **All three sit at or above the European retail reference** — so the margin argument is real and the "below cost" alarm from 23 Oct remains an artefact of the stale FX, as the 24 Oct recomputation concluded.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Turn the permit stack into a one-page compliance sheet.** Four authorities, one supplier, one set of documents. Right now no file in the workspace assembles them, which means every shipment re-discovers them. This is a **document, not a project** — high value, low cost, and it unblocks the clearing-agent conversation.
2. **Make traceability the product.** CITES permit + registered exporter code + farm origin is a *documented, compliant, farm-raised* story in a market where 96% of supply is farmed and where provenance confusion is the industry's core trust problem. Our species-name concern above is solved by leading with farm origin and CITES code, not with basin names.
3. **Confirm the duty rate.** A 20% → actual-rate correction moves every published price. It is a single lookup against the 16 Oct 2026 SARS tariff schedule.
4. **LC discipline applies here too.** The trade-desk structure and the clearing-agent email (`outbound-drafts/clearing-agent-caviar-duty-rebate.md`) are the right channel — that email is now a **confirmation, not a rescue**.

### Recommended actions

- [ ] **Build the compliance sheet this week:** CITES export permit + exporter code → DFFE permit → ITAC notification → DAFF importer registration → SARS importer code + confirmed duty line → NRCS compliance → labelling spec. One file, one owner.
- [ ] **Confirm the HS 1604.14.10 duty rate** against the SARS Schedule 1 Part 1 (16 Oct 2026) and reconcile with the 20% assumption in `caviar-landed-cost.xlsx`. One number, three prices.
- [ ] **Verify the supplier's CITES export permit and exporter registration code** before the next PO. Hard gate, no exceptions.
- [ ] **Audit all caviar marketing copy for provenance claims.** Species names yes; "Russian"/basin origin claims no.
- [ ] **Use the SA caviar import-price series** as the external benchmark in the price desk next to ICE Arabica — it is the caviar equivalent of the benchmark row we added to coffee.
