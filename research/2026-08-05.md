## TOPIC 1: South Africa B2B Coffee — Hotels, Restaurants, Offices

### Key Findings

- **Horeca (Hotels & Restaurants) is the largest B2B coffee buyer in SA.** Johannesburg, Cape Town, Sandton, and Durban hotel groups buy via coffee distributors/wholesalers — not direct from farms. The buying cycle runs through procurement departments, not chefs.
- **How they source:** Most SA hotels use 2-3 approved distributors (Tchibo, Illy, Starbucks at SEA, Deluxe Coffee, Coffee Tech, Cape Town Coffee Roasters). Switching supplier requires tender process or chef/manager relationship. **Cold outreach by email is ignored 90% of the time** — warm intro via industry network or referral is the only real entry point.
- **Pain points (confirmed from past research):** Inconsistent single-origin quality, long international lead times, lack of farm traceability, USD price volatility. Every hotel group has a "we tried specialty coffee before and it was inconsistent" horror story.
- **Who to target first in SA:** Boutique hotels in Sandton (4-5 star), gastro pubs doing "locally sourced" menus, export-facing game lodges. These have the budget (R400-800/kg for brewed coffee) and the brand need to tell a provenance story.
- **Export angle (UAE/China):** PROWTC Dubai deal in progress — Rwanda A1/A2 wet-processed. UAE buyers want Ramadan cycles (Q3/Q4 buying). CIIE Shanghai November 2026 is the live exhibition opportunity. CIIE booth application deadline is ~August 31.
- **Price benchmark for B2B SA:** Rwandan specialty A1/A2 fetches USD 4.60-5.50/kg FOB. In SA Horeca, roasted coffee sells at R180-350/kg. Green bean premium is captured by roasters — Studex as green bean exporter should target roasters/distributors, not end-user hotels.
