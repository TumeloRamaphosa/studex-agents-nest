## TOPIC 1 — COFFEE: THE BOARD MOVED AGAIN, AND CHINA'S SECOND GATE OPENED

### Key findings

**1. 🔴 The C-price has fallen another ~6.7% since our 24 Oct brief — our FOB sheet needs re-basing again.**
ICE Arabica C closed **$285.69/lb on 28 Oct 2026**, −2.74% d/d, **lowest since 9 Oct**, **−26.9% YoY** (TradingEconomics, live). Path this year: **$2.39 (Jun) → $3.56 (Jul) → $3.06 (23 Oct) → $2.89 (Oct) → $2.8569 now**. Rabobank guides **$2.50–$3.00/lb by late 2026** — we are sitting on the floor of that band. Driver is supply, not sentiment: **Brazil 2026 arabica +34.8% y/y to 48.21M bags**, Sep exports **+20% y/y to 236,000 MT**, ICE certified stocks up, and the **ICO (12 Oct) reports global consumption falling**. The Oct 19 spike to 315.70 cts/lb was short-covering and it has fully unwound.

**2. 🟢 Our $4.20–4.80/kg FOB band is actually a coherent specialty position — it just doesn't say so.** C at $285.69/lb = **$6.30/kg = $6,298/MT**. The **NAEB Best of Rwanda 2026 auction (21 Oct)** set a record: **$452/kg** top lot (Geisha, K-Organics, Huye), ~**66× the average international price**, **5× the 2025 record**; average lot **≈$205/kg**. So our sheet sits at **68–76% of C** and **2.0–2.3× the auction average** — a defensible specialty band, *if it is labelled*. Right now it is a bare number, and a CIIE buyer with a price board will do this arithmetic against us and decide we're either stale or mispriced. **Never quote $452/kg as a market price** — it is one Geisha micro-lot. Use it as the quality ceiling, and use **$205/kg (auction average)** and **$6.30/kg (C)** as the two benchmarks our band must be stated against.

**3. 🔑 China opened a SECOND gate on 20 July 2026 that our sheet does not mention.** Tariff gate: **zero tariffs from 1 May 2026** on 100% of lines from 53 African countries (two years, to ~30 Apr 2028). Phytosanitary gate: **from 20 July 2026, coffee from all 53 African countries moves under a unified phytosanitary system** — reporting: *"For Rwanda, both doors open immediately."* Africa→China coffee imports were **$246M in 2025 vs $157M in 2024**, and post-zero-tariff growth is already **+145.7%**. **We are still telling buyers one door opened when two did.** The phytosanitary unification is also a *compliance advantage* worth marketing, not just a date.

**4. 🟡 EUDR just widened — and Europe is a relationship market, not a volume market.** **Soluble coffee was added to the EUDR product scope** (European Coffee Federation, ~21 Oct). Deadlines confirmed: **large/medium + EUTR-covered = 30 Dec 2026; micro/small = 30 June 2027**, with an April 2026 Simplification Package pending clarification. The EU imports **40% of global coffee**, and the companies supplying it cover **~4/5 of the global market** (Trase, Sep 2026) — extreme concentration; Uganda sends **63%** of exports to Europe with Italy the largest importer. A new exporter does not win that market on price.

**5. 🟢 GCC is the corridor that is actually growing.** SA→UAE trade **+2.69% to ZAR 4.05bn** (Aug 2025–Aug 2026, OEC, 5 days ago); SA agricultural exports hit a record **US$4.1bn in Q2 2026, +10% YoY**; **SA biltong exports to the Middle East +14% in 2024, with UAE + Saudi = 60% of ME shipments** (Mordor, Sep 2026). Rwanda's **DUBUY** partnership (DP World B2B e-commerce, Kigali→UAE) is an existing government-backed lane into the same market.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **A price sheet that names its benchmark is worth 10 points of credibility at CIIE.** State the band as *C + differential*, with the NAEB auction average as the floor reference. Volume buyers anchor on C; specialty buyers anchor on the auction. Naming both pre-empts both.
2. **The China phytosanitary unification (20 Jul 2026) is a marketing line we are free to take and competitors have not.** "Rwanda is inside the unified phytosanitary system that China's African coffee lane now runs on" is a one-sentence differentiator for a Chinese buyer eight days from CIIE.
3. **EUDR is an opportunity hiding in a compliance burden.** Our European buyers' *importer* carries the legal obligation, but they will demand a **due-diligence statement and geolocation** from us. A ready-made DDDS file is a sales asset, and if Rwanda carries a low-risk country classification the burden is materially lighter than a high-risk origin. **Verify the classification — this is the single highest-leverage claim we could put on the Europe sheet.**
4. **GCC is where the export business is actually happening** — and we have a government-backed lane (DUBUY) into it that costs nothing to use.
