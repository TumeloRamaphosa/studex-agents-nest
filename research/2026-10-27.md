## 🔴 LEAD FINDING OF THE NIGHT

**Our Horeca sell sheet (`horeca-sell-sheet/content.json`, built 26 Jul) is an export document disguised as a domestic sell sheet — and it contains a China claim that our own research from 25 and 26 October contradicts.**

Read the actual file: it sells **green coffee at FOB USD** ("$4.20/kg", "$3.80/kg", "CIF quotes on request", MOQ 500 kg / 300 kg) to **South African hotels, restaurants, cafés and offices**. Then it says:

> *"Target: CIIE November 2026 — China Market Opening. China's coffee market is opening via CIIE (November 2026, Shanghai)."*

**Three separate failures in one document:**

1. **Wrong product for the buyer.** SA Horeca buys **roasted** beans, delivered, in ZAR, with a machine and service behind it — not green FOB from Rwanda. BeanCo: roasted to order, whole bean or ground per brewer, nationwide delivery, free samples. Café Kreme: machine rental tiered at ~60 / ~100–120 / ~200 cups per day with install, training, maintenance and insurance included. A SA hotel cannot receive a 500 kg FOB green lot from Kigali.
2. **Wrong price axis.** $4.20/kg FOB = **~R69/kg** at USD/ZAR 16.56. SA *retail* specialty is a median **R190 per 250 g = ~R756/kg** (SA Coffee Price Index Q3 2026, 39 roasters / 132 listings), entry-level ~R340/kg. We are quoting SA buyers a number **~11× below the shelf price of the thing they are actually buying** — it reads as a printing error, and it hands the negotiation to any competitor who spots it.
3. **Wrong and now-false claim.** "China's coffee market is opening via CIIE" is wrong twice: the zero-tariff treatment **opened 1 May 2026** (four months ago) and **CIIE exhibitor registration is closed**. We are telling South African hoteliers that a market opens in a month — a claim our own China buyer sheet now corrects. A prospect who reads it and later reads our China sheet loses trust in everything else on the page.

**The fix is not a rewrite. It is a split:** a **domestic roasted-and-delivered Horeca sheet** (ZAR, roasted, delivered, per-kg, with a training/service attach) and the **existing FOB green sheet** which belongs to the export track and CIIE, not to SA hotels.

## TOPIC 1 — SA B2B / HORECA COFFEE: THE BUYER, THE MODEL, THE PRICE

### Key findings

**1. The SA market is growing and the premium segment is growing twice as fast as the base.**
- SA coffee market **~USD 0.65 bn (2025)**, forecast **CAGR 4.79% (2026–2034)** (Fortune Business Insights, Oct 2026). Global market **USD 185.7 bn (2026) → USD 247.7 bn (2033)** (Mordor, Oct 2026).
- **Specialty and premium segments in SA are growing at more than double the rate of traditional instant** (IOL Business Report, Apr 2026; Bizcommunity). Two-thirds of SA adults drink coffee daily; **instant still dominates with 76% of drinkers preferring it**.
- 🔑 **Read:** the SA premium tier is the growth, and our supply story (traceable, certified, farm-direct) is exactly the premium tier's product. **But the premium buyer buys roasted, per-kg, in ZAR — the format our sheet does not speak.**

**2. 🔴 The SA specialty price band — our sell sheet does not use it.**
| Segment | Per kg (Q3 2026, SA Coffee Price Index) |
|---|---|
| Entry-level blends / commodity origin | ~**R340/kg** |
| **Median specialty 250 g bag (R190)** | ~**R756/kg** |
| Top-end competition-grade lots (e.g. Geisha) | ~**R3,196/kg** (R799/250 g ceiling) |

Retail range **R85–R799 per 250 g bag**. Our own product claims farm-direct and PROWTC certification — that argues for a **premium-tier quote (R500–R800/kg roasted wholesale-to-retail band)**, not a green FOB number. *(Note: the index explicitly excludes wholesale and 1 kg+ bulk pricing — so R756/kg is a retail anchor, and a wholesale price sits below it. Do not quote the median as a wholesale price.)*

**3. The competitive set is not other farmers — it is the rental-and-service model.**
- **Machine rental is the default entry mechanism.** Café Kreme bundles: brand-new machine, install, staff training, ongoing maintenance & technical support, insurance, cleaning chemicals, free starter coffee, upgrades as volume grows — sized at ~60 cups/day (offices) / ~100–120 (medium) / ~200 (hotels, retail, forecourts). Coffee is the consumable attached to the machine.
- **Direct-to-order roasters are the bean incumbent.** BeanCo: roasted to order in small batches, whole bean or ground to spec, nationwide delivery, free shipping >R650, **free samples up to 250 g**, custom volume tiers, one-off or recurring. Cape Coffee Beans and Bluebird run the same model; Bluebird's wholesale terms are published with free shipping thresholds.
- **Managed OCS players** named in our own Aug 9 prospects file — Dish Café, Vitago, Coffee Devices SA, Caffè Latte — are simultaneously **competitors and distribution channels**. Our file's conclusion still stands: *none of them offer barista training.* **Training is the wedge.** A roaster sells beans; an OCS sells machines and beans; a training-attached supplier sells a **retainer**.

**4. Our own buyer list is decent but has never been worked, and it is missing the segment that matters most.**
`sa-b2b-coffee-prospects.md` (9 Aug) lists 10 hotels, 10 cafés/restaurants, 4 OCS players across JHB/CPT. **Gaps:** no **KZN/Durban** venues (Studex Meat is Durban-based — The Oyster Box is on the list but alone), no **corporate offices** (the ~60-cup/day tier), no **guest houses/lodges** (Origin Coffee SA claims supply to 100+ cafés, restaurants, guest houses, offices and hotels — the incumbents already sell that route), no **coffee-machine rental** competitors as a distinct list.

**5. 🟡 A real, dated risk on the South Africa side: certification claims must survive a procurement audit.**
Our sheet leans on PROWTC, Rainforest Alliance, Organic and EUDR-adjacent traceability language. Note our own 26 Oct finding: **we hold no plot-geolocation data** (we buy through a washing station). For a domestic SA buyer, EUDR is not the gate — but **a hotel group's sustainability questionnaire is.** A certification claim that cannot be evidenced on request is a liability in a procurement cycle, not a selling point.

**6. Biltong/meat price benchmark — same question, now answerable from a public source.**
For the price desk (the 24 Oct "price reality check" work): competitor SA bands are **A/B-grade sliced/slabs R349.99–449.99/kg · C-grade geelvet R399.99–499.99/kg · A/B drywors R349.99–449.99/kg · wagyu biltong/drywors R499.99–599.99/kg · biltong sticks R599.99–699.99/kg (up to R999.99/kg in small snack packs)**. Differentiators the market rewards: premium cuts without trimmings, grass-fed/hormone-free supply, clean facility, **free 1–4 day delivery over R1,000**, and **automated bulk discount tiers** (3+ = 10%, 5+ = 15%, 10+ = 20%). Our live Shopify listing shows wagyu biltong at **R12,500 (from R16,500)** — a different unit class entirely; confirm whether that is a whole-slab or per-kg figure before it appears next to a competitor table.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Split the sheet, don't rewrite the business.** Domestic roasted-and-delivered (ZAR, roasted, per-kg, machine-fit grinds, training attach, sample-first) is one document. The FOB green + China export sheet stays exactly where it belongs — the export track.
2. **Price on the SA premium band, not on green parity.** Our supply chain supports a **R500–R800/kg roasted wholesale** position in the premium tier, and "30–40% below distributor" becomes a *checkable* claim only if we benchmark against SA roasted wholesale, not against green FOB.
3. **Lead with training, follow with beans.** Sample box → cupping with the team → roasted supply contract → recurring training retainer. That is the only sequence that turns a one-off sample into a recurring revenue line, and it is the one thing the OCS incumbents do not do.
4. **Attack Durban/KZN and the corporate-office tier.** Offices are the lowest-friction SA Horeca buyer (no chef, no menu, monthly recurring, ~60 cups/day) and we have zero of them. Durban is our own doorstep.
5. **Make the certifications evidence-backed or drop them from the domestic sheet.** A one-page traceability dossier (PROWTC certificate, chain-of-custody, farm origin) that a procurement team can verify is worth more than four certification logos.
6. **Reuse the channel we already paid to build.** Blotato DM automation + AgentMail + Discord war-room is a working outreach stack. The SA Horeca sell sheet is a document we have never sent to anyone.

- [ ] **Delete the "China's coffee market is opening via CIIE" block from any SA-facing page.** It is contradicted by our own China sheet and by CIIE's own registration status.
- [ ] **Replace the FOB price column with a SA roasted price band** derived from the SA Coffee Price Index, with the retail/wholesale distinction stated so the buyer cannot accuse us of quoting retail as wholesale.
- [ ] **Build the roasted-and-delivered service offer:** grind-to-machine spec, minimum order, delivery radius from Durban, sample box (up to 500 g free, mirroring the market standard), and a stated monthly minimum.
- [ ] **Add the training attach explicitly** to the sheet — this is our differentiator against Dish Café / Vitago / Coffee Devices SA, and it should be a named line, not a bullet.
- [ ] **Extend the prospect list with 20 KZN + corporate-office names** (Durban, Umhlanga, Umzumbe, Richards Bay, Port Elizabeth corridor; office managers and F&B managers as the contact titles). We have 24 SA names from 9 Aug and have contacted none.

**2. 🔴 Blotato's underlying platforms are closing, and two of the closures land on our live publish path.**
Blotato's own Social Media API Report 2026 catalogues **34 breaking changes** (13 directly impacting posting) and **31 version sunsets** across 10 platforms. What matters to us:
- **X (Twitter) — our quoting/publishing channel:**
  - **Pay-per-use only.** $0.015 per regular post; **$0.200 per post containing a URL**; $0.010 for summoned replies. Legacy Basic/Pro sunset **1 Jun and 1 Sep 2026**; subscribers were auto-migrated to credits. **Opting out terminates access.**
  - **Programmatic replies are now summoned-only** (since 23 Feb 2026) — replies permitted only when the original author @mentions or quotes us. **Any DM/comment auto-reply strategy on X is dead.**
  - **Automated engagement is gone entirely** (since 20 Apr 2026): API creation of Following, Likes and Quote-Posts removed on all self-serve tiers. `POST /2/users/:id/likes` and `/following` removed from the Free tier (22 Aug 2025).
- **Meta / Instagram / Facebook:**
  - **Facebook Messenger Page tags now fail with `Error 100`** (since 27 Apr 2026): `CONFIRMED_EVENT_UPDATE`, `ACCOUNT_UPDATE`, `POST_PURCHASE_UPDATE`. Replacement path is **Utility Templates / Marketing Messages API**. → *If any Studex flow still posts a Messenger tag, it is silently failing.*
  - **Removed metrics** — `impressions`, `plays`, Reels play counts (21 Apr 2025); `video_views`, `email_contacts`, `profile_views`, `website_clicks` (8 Jan 2025); Facebook Page Insights `impressions` and `page fans` (15 Nov 2025). → *Any dashboard or report we show using these fields is reading dead numbers.*
  - **Instagram Basic Display API is fully shut down** (4 Dec 2024); v1.0 Marketing API endpoints deprecated (20 May 2025).
- **Threads:** posts with **>5 links fail container creation** (`THREADS_API__LINK_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`, 22 Dec 2025). Tenor GIF support removed; **GIPHY replaces it**.
- **Reddit:** new public Data API access requests close **31 Oct 2026** (4 days away) → unregistered apps begin losing access **12 Jan 2027** → full shutdown **March 2027**. We do not post to Reddit (Blotato does not either), so this is informational.
- **Bluesky: 0 breaking changes documented** — video/image limits raised, OAuth stabilised. **The only platform in the set with a clean bill of health.**

**3. The failure mode to fear is not an error — it is silence.** Blotato's own finding: **apps posting on behalf of other users face mandatory audits across 9 of 10 platforms** (all but Bluesky; X additionally requires review for AI-generated content), and **unreviewed apps on TikTok and Pinterest (Trial tier) have their posts locked to private visibility — the integration appears functional in testing while remaining invisible to the public.** A post pipeline that fails *open* (queue accepts, publish silently private) is worse than one that fails loudly, because our dashboards would report success.

**4. Higgsfield — usable, but it is a creative suite, not our differentiator.** October 2026 changelog: **Supercomputer (22 Oct 2026)**, Content Factory A, Ideogram Vector 1.0, Kimi K3.1 Pro (Moonshot open-weight) support, character-type grading. Cinema Studio 4.0 (real camera direction), Genjutsu (edit without reshooting), Seedance 2.5 / Wan 3.0 (30-second clips), Ad tooling. Pricing: **Starter 200 credits/mo · Plus 1,000 · Ultra 3,000.** 🔑 **Verdict: we already have a full video-generation stack** (MiniMax-H3/H3-Max/Hailuo via `submit_video_generation`, plus `gen_videos`) and a content pipeline. Higgsfield earns its place only where **character consistency across a campaign** matters — i.e. a recurring brand presenter for the coffee or biltong feed. **Do not buy a subscription this month.** Revisit when a specific campaign needs a consistent human/character across 10+ assets, and price it against Ultra credits at that point.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **The automation verdict is unchanged and now fully evidenced: self-host n8n.** Browser Use + MCP on our own VM gives a competitor-price watcher, a listing-price watcher, and a lead-routing pipeline at zero marginal platform cost. Make stays out.

4. **Bluesky is free headroom** — the only platform with zero breaking changes and raised limits. Low priority, but it is a cheap new surface for the brand feed while the others get more expensive.
5. **Treat "publishes but is invisible" as a first-class failure.** Add a post-publish visibility check to the pipeline: confirm the post is publicly readable, not merely accepted. That is a small guard against a large, invisible loss.
6. **Higgsfield: no purchase.** Revisit against a specific campaign brief, priced at Ultra (3,000 credits/mo).
