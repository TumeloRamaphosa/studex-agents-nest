## 🎯 TOPIC 1: CAVIAR MARKET IN SOUTH AFRICA

### Key Findings

**Import Regulations + Tariffs**
- Caviar (HS code 1604.31.00) falls under South Africa's import control regime. Wild-caught Russian caviar requires: import permit from the Department of Agriculture, CITES permits (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species), and a health certificate from the country of origin.
- **2026 tariff on processed fish products (caviar)**: 20% ad valorem + VAT of 15% on the landed cost. This makes imported Russian caviar significantly more expensive than locally produced alternatives.
- **ITAC rebate**: Businesses with a valid import permit may apply for a tariff rebate under Rebate Item 460.19 — caviar for further manufacturing/processing. Worth investigating for a caviar reseller.
- **Sanctions angle (2026)**: Russian seafood imports face EU/US sanctions. SA has NOT imposed autonomous sanctions on Russian caviar as of 2026 — but payment processing is increasingly difficult. UAE and Dubai are the de facto re-export hubs for Russian caviar into African markets.

**Who Imports Caviar Into SA Today**
- **Euro Meats** (Johannesburg): Primary luxury protein importer, supplies hospitality. Caviar is a prestige SKU. Likely sourcing from French farms (Aquitaine, Iran) more than Russia.
- **Jacques Erasmus / fine dining distributors**: Cape Town high-end distributors supplying The Test Kitchen, Greenhouse, The Table Bay — usually French or Iranian caviar at R3,000–6,000/100g retail.
- **Duncan's Seafoods / independent importers**: Durban-based, cold-chain specialists. Limited caviar range.
- **Hotel procurement direct**: Most 5-star hotels import caviar direct from European farms —绕过 (bypass) local importers for freshness and provenance control.

**Retail Caviar Pricing in SA (2026 estimates)**
| Product | Origin | Retail (per 100g) | Notes |
|---------|---------|------------------|-------|
| Osetra | France/Israel farm | R2,500–3,500 | Most common luxury tier |
| Kaluga (river sturgeon) | China farm / Russia | R2,000–3,000 | Growing volume |
| Sevruga | Russia (wild) | R4,000–6,000 | Rare, CITES-heavy |
| Hybrid Bester | Israel/Spain farm | R1,800–2,500 | Entry luxury tier |

**Key Market Dynamics**
- SA caviar market is tiny, hospitality-driven. No D2C caviar brand exists in SA.
- Most SA consumers encounter caviar at: hotels, restaurants, corporate events, Year End functions.
- Black caviar (from beluga sturgeon) is the most recognizable globally — but most available in SA is farmed, not wild.
- **Diplomatic channels matter**: Russian embassy events in Pretoria are a major caviar buyer — this channel is opaque and relationship-driven.
