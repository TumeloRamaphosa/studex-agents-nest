## TOPIC 1 — Coffee: Who is buying African coffee, and what does it mean for Studex

### Key findings

- **China is the single biggest live opportunity and the window is open now.** Zero-tariff on 100% of tariff lines for 53 African countries effective 1 May 2026 (green bean 8% → 0%, roasted 15% → 0%). From 20 July 2026 a unified phytosanitary system allows qualified green beans from all 53 origins (diplomatic ties required). **African coffee exports to China are up +145.7% YoY (Jan–May 2026).** China coffee demand growing 30%+ annually. *(Note: the non-LDC zero-tariff window for SA/Kenya/Nigeria/Egypt expires 30 Apr 2028; LDC status for Rwanda/Burundi has no stated expiry — Rwanda is our better lane.)*
- **The value-add prize is roasting, not green.** Green beans ~$3.80/kg FOB; **roasted $8.50/kg FOB (+124% margin uplift, ~$4.70/kg retained in Africa)**. Cocoa, cashew and spice margins are similar (+96% to +167%). We should not be exporting green.
- **Compliance is the gate, and it's document-specific.** Certificate of Origin must explicitly reference the China zero-tariff preference framework (generic FTA form = rejected, reverted to 8–30% MFN), plus phytosanitary certificate in Chinese-translated format, health cert for processed foods, 10-digit HS code match, and CIQ/SFDA registration for animal products + halal certificate for meat.
- **Counterparty risk is real.** Unvetted small trading desks are the top fraud risk in the new African→China lane. Recommended payment: confirmed Letters of Credit + Trade Assurance, no open accounts.
- **Europe is the harder, higher-margin lane — gated by EUDR.** Coffee must be deforestation-free with a **31 Dec 2020 cut-off date**; large operators face enforcement **30 Dec 2026**, SMEs **30 Jun 2027**. EU FOB for African specialty: **€12–18/kg green, €18–22/kg with a buyer relationship.** Compliance gaps persist for smallholders (Daily Coffee News, 7 Oct 2026) — traceability is the moat, not the bean.
- **UAE is a re-export hub, not an end market.** UAE raw coffee bean market USD 314m (2025) → 332m (2026) → 446m (2032). Rwanda ran a cupping/trade tour in Abu Dhabi, Dubai, Sharjah 9–11 Oct 2026 — UAE is used as a trade-show platform for African origins, not as the consumer. Middle East buyers (Luckin-style) want **roasted or private-label product**, which aligns with our highest-margin lane.
- **Price context.** Arabica C ~**$3.09–3.14/lb** (21–22 Oct 2026), down 22.5% YoY from $4.05; 2026 average $3.01 vs $3.67 in 2025. 52-week range $2.44–4.23. → **Buyer leverage is high this quarter; prices favour importers, and "premium African origin" positioning must be sold on story/traceability, not scarcity.** We cannot sell on tight supply.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Export roasted, not green.** Moving our African green → roasted in-country adds ~$4.70/kg. This is the single largest margin lever in the research.
2. **CIIE (Shanghai, this week) is the cheapest possible distribution test.** China is 0% tariff + +145.7% growth + we have 9 buyer emails already sent and 21 promised meetings we can't evidence. Use the buyers we have; don't cold-start the market.
3. **Rwanda-first lane, not SA lane.** Rwanda's LDC zero-tariff has no stated expiry while the non-LDC window (incl. SA) closes 30 Apr 2028. Our Rwanda A1 sample pricing is already in circulation.
4. **UAE = regional showcase + re-export door**, not a volume market. Best use: buyer meetings, private-label roasted coffee for Gulf cafés, and using Dubai as a hub for African beans moving onward.
5. **"Document-first" as a service.** Most African exporters are losing the 0% tariff on paperwork errors. A packaging/checklist offer (CO + phyto + health cert + CIQ/halal) is a cheap differentiator we could attach to any deal — and we already have a halal certification for the meat side.

### Recommended actions

- **Before CIIE:** produce a **China Compliance Pack** (CO wording referencing the preference framework, phyto cert template in Chinese-translated format, 10-digit HS codes, product registration checklist) and attach it to every one of the 21 promised buyer meetings. This is what wins the meeting, not the price.
- **Fix the spec sheet:** sell **roasted** at $8.50/kg FOB anchor with green at $3.80/kg as the floor; publish both so buyers can see the uplift we pass on.
- **Payment policy from day one:** confirmed LC only. No open accounts, no first-time-desk buyers.
- **UAE:** after CIIE, target 5 Gulf roasters/café chains + 1 private-label prospect, pitch roasted private-label (no green).
- **EU:** don't chase EU until we have EUDR geolocation + traceability for the specific lot. Note the 30 Dec 2026 large-operator date as a *customer* selling point: we can be EUDR-ready before our competitors.

## TOPIC 2 — South Africa B2B coffee: buyers, sourcing behaviour, pain points

### Key findings

- **Market size and growth.** SA coffee beans market ~USD 0.65bn (2025), CAGR 4.79% (2026–2034). Global B2B coffee channels growing 4.9% CAGR. **95 wholesale coffee suppliers headquartered in SA as of Sep 2026** — this is a crowded, fragmented field where a 28-day-old brand will not win on beans alone.
- **The buying decision is not about coffee.** SA businesses (offices, cafés, hotels, guesthouses) select suppliers on five criteria: (1) consistency of taste batch-to-batch, (2) complete solutions — beans **plus** machines, consumables, staff/barista training, (3) machine uptime, (4) reliable delivery cadence with predictable stock, (5) after-sales support that answers the phone.
- **Documented pain points (verbatim supplier-industry consensus):** inconsistent quality week to week; "cheap" coffee becoming expensive via poor machine compatibility and breakdowns; equipment downtime halting service; stockouts and late deliveries; suppliers vanishing after the sale.
- **Equipment matching is by segment:** bean-to-cup automatic machines for offices, high-volume setups for cafés, industrial/high-capacity for factories and warehouses (corporatecoffee.co.za product line, Oct 2026). Volume question buyers ask first: *cups per day*.
- **Competitive field:** national roasters (Coffee Warehouse, Miko/Puro — "no contracts, no faff" positioning), machine vendors selling beans as a loss-leader (Buna, Dallmayr, König, Coffee Pro Direct), and specialists like Haas, Supply Chain Co., Bluebird, Taste of Africa, EURIPA. **Miko's "no contracts" angle and the machine-vendor bundling model are the two threats to copy.**
- **Price anchor:** wholesale roaster COGS commonly ~46%, ~54% gross margin. Any B2B offer under ~50% margin is a mistake.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **We have a coffee + protein cross-sell no bean roaster has.** Competitors sell beans and machines. **We can sell beans + biltong + caviar + gifting** — a hospitality buyer gets coffee, a VIP welcome-pack gets biltong, a wine-pairing menu gets caviar. This is the only structurally defensible angle in a market with 95 suppliers.
2. **In-room / welcome-pack sachet programme** for the 10 hotels already on our prospect list (Saxon, Four Seasons Westcliff, Table Bay, Cape Grace, Oyster Box, Ellerman House…). Low volume per SKU, high margin, and it creates a captive recurring bean relationship.
3. **"No contracts, no faff" is copyable and beats the corporate roasters.** A pain-point-led message (consistent roast, machine uptime, delivery promise, barista training) outperforms a price-led one. We can say it credibly because we're not a legacy roaster.
4. **Barista training as a lead magnet** — Saturday-morning workshops for premium independents (Post, Haas, Bcrafted, Stockies) convert a supplier conversation into a relationship.
5. **Factories, warehouses and showrooms** (corporatecoffee.co.za's industrial line) are a low-competition segment in SA with predictable, contract-friendly volume.

### Recommended actions

- **Rebuild the B2B proposition around pain points, not product:** "Consistent roast. Machine uptime. Delivery that doesn't fail you. And a protein brand your guests remember." One landing page, one sell sheet — kill the generic bean catalogue.
- **Package the bundle as the hero SKU:** Biltong + coffee gift box / hotel welcome pack / corporate gifting with coffee (we already have corporate-gifting and custom-box-builder assets).
- **Set a floor price** from the 54% gross-margin benchmark before we quote anything.
- **One target segment at a time:** start with 5 premium independents + 3 hotels in JHB/CPT, not 95 suppliers' worth of market. The current 10-hotel + 10-café + OCS prospect list (sa-b2b-coffee-prospects.md) is the right size — execute it rather than expand it.
- **Say it in their language:** cups/day, machine model, water quality, delivery cycle. Our sell sheets should lead with volume + machine, not flavour notes.
