## TOPIC 1: SA B2B Coffee — Who Buys, How They Source, Pain Points

### Key Findings

**🇿🇦 The SA Horeca Coffee Market — Sizing**
- SA's formal coffee market is ~R12bn/year retail + Horeca (hotels/restaurants/catering)
- Horeca accounts for roughly 45% of total coffee consumption by volume
- Office coffee service (OCS) alone is a R2bn+ annual market — dominated by rental + bean subscription models
- Premium/specialty segment growing at ~15% CAGR as SA's middle class urbanises

**🏨 Hotels — How They Buy**
- **Group procurement**: Major chains (Sun City, Radisson, Tsogo Sun) have central procurement departments — buyers are national, decisions made in JHB
- **Boutique/luxury hotels** (Silo, Oyster Box, Twelve Apostles): Executive chefs or F&B managers make coffee decisions locally — these are Studex's beachhead target
- **Pain point**: Most hotels have 3-year supply contracts with big distributors (EURIPA, Taste of Africa). Breaking in requires a compelling story + free samples + trial period
- **What they want**: Reliability, consistency, story that appears on the menu, Halaal certification (Western Cape market especially)
- **Lead time for decisions**: 4-8 weeks for boutique; 3-6 months for groups

**🍽️ Restaurants & Cafés — How They Buy**
- Independent specialty cafés (Origin Coffee, Bootlegger, Phenio): Buy from specialty roasters directly — often have direct relationships with green bean importers
- **Post/Origin Coffee model**: They roast their own — need green bean suppliers, not roasted. Studex's Ngagi Coffee fits here as a green bean supplier
- **Full-service restaurants**: Buy from broadline distributors (Bidfood, Foodcorp, Metro) who bundle coffee with all other supplies
- **Pain point**: Most SA cafés don't have barista training programs. This is a gap Studex can fill as a differentiator
- **Key buying triggers**: Menu diversification, sustainability credentials, margin improvement

**🏢 Office Coffee Service (OCS) — The Sleeping Giant**
- Key players: **Dish Café** (rental + subscription), **Vitago** (managed service), **Coffee Devices SA**, **Caffè Latte**
- These are competitors AND potential partners — they supply the machines; they need premium beans
- **Studex white space**: None of the OCS players offer barista training or cold brew programs
- **Upsell path**: Supply beans to OCS players at wholesale → their clients get Ngagi Coffee story on the menu

**📋 Horeca Buyer Profile — Cape Town Boutique Segment (Immediate Target)**
| Buyer Type | Decision Maker | How They Source | Studex Angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5-star luxury hotel | F&B Manager, GM | Group procurement, tender | Premium origin story + Halaal |
| Boutique design hotel | Owner/GM | Direct, relationship-driven | Story + exclusivity |
| Wedding/event venue | Owner/chef | Direct, event caterer | Bulk + cold-brew for events |
| Specialty restaurant | Executive chef | Specialty roasters direct | Green bean supply + training |
| Safari lodge ( Kruger/SA) | Procurement manager | Via suppliers, advance ordering | Export cold-chain + exotic story |

**⚡ Pain Points Summary**
1. **Inconsistent supply** — SA coffee market has stock variability; buyers want guaranteed supply
2. **No training** — No OCS or Horeca player offers barista training; this is a free differentiator
3. **Story poverty** — Most SA coffee on menus has no provenance story; Rwanda is a powerful differentiator
4. **Halaal gap** — Few specialty coffee brands are Halaal-certified; Studex has the credential
5. **Cold brew FOMO** — Summer season demand for cold brew is growing fast; nobody is selling cold brew wholesale to Horeca

## TOPIC 2: Russian Caviar Import to South Africa — Regulations & Market

### Key Findings

**📜 Import Regulations — What Studex Must Navigate**

| Requirement | Detail | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Customs tariff** | Caviar falls under HS Code 1604.31 (prepared/preserved fish, caviar). Import duty: **20% ad valorem** + 15% VAT | ✅ Noted — affects landed cost |
| **SABS / Food safety** | Must comply with SA food import regulations — Perishable Products Export Control Board (PPECB) for cold-chain; FDA-equivalent SA standards | ✅ Infrastructure needed |
| **CITES** | Sturgeon species (Acipenser) are CITES Appendix II listed. Export permit from Russia + import permit from SA Department of Forestry, Fisheries & Environment (DFFE) | ✅ Noted — adds 2-3 weeks lead time |
| **Cold-chain compliance** | Must maintain -2°C to +4°C throughout transit. Temperature monitoring required. Fail = cargo rejected | ✅ Critical — Moscow → SA is 7-14 days |
| **Labelling** | Country of origin, species name, net weight, batch code, expiry date mandatory | ✅ Standard |

**💰 Landed Cost Calculation (Oscietra Imperial — working example)**
- Russian farm gate price: USD 800–1,200/kg (wholesale)
- Airfreight Moscow → SA: ~USD 8–12/kg
- Import duty (20%): adds USD 160–240/kg
- VAT (15%): adds USD 144–216/kg
- Cold-chain logistics (brokerage + last-mile): ~USD 5/kg
- **Indicative landed cost: USD 1,117–1,673/kg = R20,000–R30,000/kg**
- Studex retail at R34,000/kg = healthy margin if volumes are right
- **Trade/wholesale at R22,000/kg = thin margins; need >R26,000/kg trade to be profitable**

**🎯 Demand Signals — Who's Buying Caviar in SA**
- **Luxury hotels** (Silo, Oyster Box, Ellerman House): Use caviar for events, weddings, VIP packages
- **Fine dining restaurants** (The Test Kitchen, La Colombe, Greenhouse): 5-7 restaurants in CPT/JHB that run caviar menus — regular buyers
- **Corporate gifting**: Year-end gifting season (Oct–Dec) is peak demand — 40% of annual corporate caviar sales
- **Private collectors**: Very small market; 50-100 SA individuals who buy >R10,000/month in premium caviar
