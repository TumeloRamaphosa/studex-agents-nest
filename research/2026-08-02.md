## Topic 1: African Coffee Export Markets — UAE, China, Europe

**Key Findings:**
- 🌍 **UAE is Africa's biggest re-export hub:** Dubai ports handle massive coffee transit trade — duty-free, world-class logistics, gravitating to GCC, Africa, Asia. Premium African beans (Ethiopian Sidamo, Kenyan AA) command premium prices here. UAE buyers = trading houses (Al Ghurair, Al Maya), HORECA importers, and specialty roasters.
- ☕ **China's coffee addiction is accelerating:** Zero-tariff policy (May 2025) for 53 African diplomatic partners = Ethiopian, Kenyan, Tanzanian beans now tariff-free into China. Starbucks China has 7,000+ stores, all competing for premium Arabica. China's middle class is the single biggest growth opportunity for African coffee over the next decade.
- 🇪🇺 **Europe: Germany & Italy are grinders, not growers' friends:** Germany (Hamburg) is the world's largest green coffee import hub, but re-exports roasted/ground. Italy (Trieste) dominates specialty espresso trade. European buyers pay premium for certification (Rainforest Alliance, Fairtrade) — Ethiopian and Rwandan beans doing well here. Nordic roasters (Tim Wendelboe, Johan Johansson) driving single-origin premium trends.
- 💰 **Prices:** Specialty African Arabica fetching $3–8/lb FOB (vs $1.40 commodity Robusta). Ethiopia's foreign exchange crisis = push to sell faster = price opportunity for buyers. Green coffee at multi-year highs (ICO composite up 40%+ since 2023).
- 📦 **South Africa as a coffee export gateway?** SA has no significant coffee export — we import everything. But SA could be a roasting and packaging hub for African-origin beans destined for China (via existing shipping lanes) — but would need green bean sourcing agreements.

**Opportunities for Studex:**
- Source Ethiopian/Kenyan green beans → roast locally → sell to SA B2B market AND explore China OEM channel
- China zero-tariff is a structural tailwind — if SA can get coffee to Shanghai at competitive freight, it's a real opportunity
- UAE HORECA market is reachable via existing trade lanes — could be a faster test market

## Topic 2: Voicebox — AI Voice Stack for Customer Service

**Key Findings:**
- 🔊 **What it is:** Open-source AI voice studio (voicebox.sh) by Jamie Pine. Full voice I/O stack running locally — voice cloning, TTS, speech-to-text dictation, voice agent support. MIT licensed.
- 🎙️ **Core capabilities:** Clone any voice from 30 seconds of audio. Generate speech in cloned voices. Dictate into any app. Talk to AI agents in your own voice. Full local/offline operation — no cloud dependency.
- 🔌 **For Studex use cases:** Could power WhatsApp voice input for orders (customer speaks order → transcribed → routed to Charlie/Naledi). Could give the Studex website a "voice concierge" that speaks in a branded voice. Could enable voice notes to agents instead of typing.
- ⚠️ **Constraints:** Self-hosted (needs compute). No native WhatsApp integration yet — would need to bridge via Twilio or similar. Still v1 — actively developing. CPU-only inference is slow; GPU recommended.
- 📍 **Competition:** ElevenLabs (cloud, more mature), Resemble.ai, Coqui — but Voicebox's local-first + MIT license makes it attractive for data privacy-sensitive uses.

**Opportunities for Studex:**
- **High-effort, high-reward** — not a 1-hour build
- Best immediate use: Clone Naledi's voice for Auto Meat customer service voicemails — warm, SA brand feel
- Secondary: WhatsApp voice order intake for caviar/biltong — customers speak what they want
- Long-term: AI voice agent for website — handles FAQs while you sleep
