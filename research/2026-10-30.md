## TOPIC 1 — COFFEE EXPORT: WHO IS BUYING, AND WHAT THEY PAY

### Key findings

**1. 🔴 China is now fully open to us, and it opened in two steps — so the barrier is no longer China, it is us.**
- From **1 May 2026** China eliminated tariffs on all taxable products from **53 African countries** (Shanghai gov't / China Daily).
- From **20 July 2026** a **single continent-wide approval regime** lets qualified coffee beans from all 53 countries in (Barista Magazine; CGTN). No country-by-country fight.
- **China imported $246m of African coffee in 2025, up from $157m in 2024 (+57%)** (China-Africa Business Council). Total China coffee imports **$837m** (USDA GAIN, Jan 2026).
- **Shanghai Port handles 28% of China's green-bean imports; Kunshan produces ~60% of China's roasted coffee** (USDA GAIN, Aug 2026). The buyer you want is within a truck of Kunshan.

**2. 🟡 The competition for the Rwanda lane is not other exporters — it is Rwanda's own government channel and one Chinese roaster.**
- **Rwanda signed an MoU with Cotti Coffee**, a Chinese coffee value chain, to boost production and expand into China (CIIE, Jun 2025).
- Rwanda showcased coffee at **Café Show China 2026 (Beijing, Aug)** with producers from 20+ countries; earlier Alibaba campaigns moved **1.5 t in seconds** and 3,000+ bags in under a minute (RDB).
- **Mauritius is making its CIIE debut (5–10 Nov) with a China-first story: 50,651 bags / 3,039 t already exported to China.** That is a country with a *coherent national China narrative* arriving at the same show we are.
- **Ethiopia is the volume wall**: 470,000 t exported in 2024/25 = $2.65bn; **48,000 t to China in FY2025/26**; China is its 3rd-largest market; **Tracon alone shipped >10,000 t to China in 2025**. Kenya ~50,000 t/yr.
- 💡 **We are not competing with Ethiopia on tonnage. We are competing on a 5 MT custom-blend lot with published cupping and lot traceability — a size Ethiopia cannot service.**

**3. 🟢 The Gulf is a re-export hub, not just a buyer — and it is the cheapest gate into Asia.** UAE green import prices **$2.50–6.50/kg** (IndexBox, Sep 2026). Dubai is the transhipment/re-export node into MENA, and **World of Coffee Dubai 2026** (5th edition) is the Middle East's premier specialty platform. MEA coffee market **$17.14bn (2026) → $24.89bn (2031), 7.75% CAGR** (Mordor). Saudi Arabia is #2 in Ethiopia's destination list after Germany.

**4. 🟡 Europe is a different, worse business than we think — it is a price/differentiation market, not a volume market.** CBI: *"The European coffee market is saturated… European Arabica imports are expected to remain stable in the 2025-2026 season."* Europe Arabica market ≈ **$10.97bn (2026)**. African origin's real European anchors are **Germany, Belgium, the UK** — and the route is **EUDR (large/medium 30 Dec 2026)**. We are a small exporter; EUDR is a paperwork cost we should treat as a *filter*, not a market.

**5. 🟡 The whole trade is price-led, so the differential is the product.** Global coffee export **value +39% in 2025 while volume fell 2.4%** — value up on price, not tonnage. Green is priced as **ICE "C" + a differential** for grade, screen size, certification and origin. Our published **cupping score (A1 17+ / 84+; A2 15–16)** *is* the differential, and it is the one asset a commodity exporter cannot fake. ⚠️ *Our A1 band of $4,200–4,800/MT vs A2 $3,600–4,000/MT is a $600–1,000/MT gap — that gap is the cupping score being monetised, and it must be stated as such on the CIIE card.*

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Two doors, one product.** China (zero tariff + continent-wide approval + Kunming/Kunshan buyers) and the Gulf (Dubai re-export, low price band, MEA growth). Same bag, same FOB basis, two buyer lists.
2. **Sell the small lot to the large roaster.** 5 MT custom blend with published score and lot traceability is a *different product* from Ethiopia's container flow. Compete on **flexibility + traceability**, never on price.
3. **Dubai is a reselling position, not a terminal one.** A Dubai buyer (PROWTC is one) is a channel into MENA and, from there, Asia — but the **deal must be the right buyer**: PROWTC's document is a proposal *from* us *to* them, and it carries a dead email.
4. **CIIE is a buyer-matching event, not a showcase.** The money at CIIE is in the pre-booked buyer list, not the stand. 6 days is enough to send a one-pager to 20 named importers.

**Our "budget" tier is R900/kg and our "premium" tier is R1,450/kg, against a market that ends at R700/kg.** Every one of those numbers is a trade-buyer objection we cannot answer with a product story.

**2. 🔴 The competitors win on *format* and *mechanics*, not on meat.** What they do that we do not:
- **Brooklyn Biltong: made in the US, "no import"** — they manufacture locally to dodge freight and duty, and sell **$13.31 per 3.2 oz bag ($3.10–3.66/oz ≈ $110–130/kg)** with a **subscription** ($33.96). They compete on convenience + repeat purchase, and they have removed the import cost from their model entirely.
- **Ember: a bite-sized "shot" format** — biltong reformatted for the snack occasion, not the protein occasion (Dataintelo, 2025).
- **Lowfeld Soul Food: SA-run, made to order, shipped within days** — *freshness as the moat*.
- **Kalahari, Ayoba, Stryve, Righteous Felon, Vacadillos** hold the US national shelf.
- **Competitors publish our comparison pages.** `btbiltong.com`, `yebobiltong.com`, `brooklynbiltong.com`, `jerkybrands.com` all run "best biltong brands in the US (2026)" and "vs other online brands" content. **They are running our SEO. We have no equivalent content and no comparator page.**

**3. 🟢 The category is being globalised right now, and the Western press is doing our PR for free.** **The Economist, 23 Sep 2026: "Biltong is going global—like South Africans."** Biltong market **USD 272m (2025) → 471.4m (2034), 6.3% CAGR** (GrowthMarketReports); biltong *snack* market **$460m (2025) → $845.7m (2034), 7.0% CAGR**, led by South Africa (Dataintelo). ⚠️ *Two different market definitions, same order of magnitude — use as directional, not as a citable number.*

**4. 🟡 The SA export channel has volume headroom but the beef line is under pressure.** SA cumulative beef/sheep/goat exports **2025→May 2026 ≈ 81,000 t / R63bn**; key markets **China, Egypt, UAE, Jordan**; leading destinations **UK, Netherlands, UAE**. But **SA total beef exports fell 26% in 2025 despite growing global demand.** 💡 **Volume is not the play. A premium *snack-positioned* SKU at a defensible price is.**

**5. 🔴 Corporate gifting is owned by someone else.** Biltong Boytjies owns the "corporate gift basket" keyword with a dedicated product page; biltong.co.za runs gifts + tiered cakes (Biltong Cake 1 Tier R1,200); gifting is the *cheapest* to sell and the *most* contested. Our own biltong ladder (R295–R720 hampers) is **cheaper than our own corporate-gifting page** — a differentiator we are not using.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Fix the ladder before we sell anything else.** The 7 kg case must be **cheaper per kg than 7 × 1 kg** — that is the whole trade proposition, and today it is 23% the wrong way. And the 500 g must not cost more per kg than the 1 kg. *(Price changes need the Shopify Admin token, which is still 401 — so tonight this is a spec, not a live edit.)*
2. **Stop competing on meat, compete on format and origin.** "Wagyu 500 g at R900/kg" is indefensible in a market where Woolworths sells sliced biltong at R700/kg. The defensible premium is **Wagyu + a format (thin-cut snack pack, chilli-bites, 100 g resealable) + traceability + gifting** — Ember's shot format is the cheapest version of this idea.
3. **Two mechanical moves we can copy without new capability:** (a) **a subscription** — we already have `studex-biltong/subscription.html` in source; check whether it is live; (b) **a comparator page** — publish *our own* "best SA biltong brands (2026)" and "Studex vs other online brands" pages, because three competitors are already occupying that space against us.
4. **The Economist piece is a press asset.** A "SA biltong is going global" narrative attached to our origin story is free credibility for the export lane and for CIIE.
5. **One account, two products.** A corporate-gifting buyer takes biltong *and* coffee — we have both, and our coffee B2B sheet (live since 29 Oct) is already a quotable document.
