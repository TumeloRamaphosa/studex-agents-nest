## Topic 1: SA B2B Coffee — Market Intel + China Opportunity

### Key Findings

**China opens to ALL 54 African coffee markets — EFFECTIVE JULY 2026.**
- Beijing announced bilateral agreements covering all African countries, no longer requiring China-origin re-export
- This fundamentally changes the Rwanda→China direct export calculus — no more middleman in Kenya/Tanzania
- Rwanda coffee landed in China: ~$6–8/kg green bean premium (FOB), sells at 3–5x in Chinese specialty cafés
- South Africa now has a window as a logistics hub AND as a B2B buyer between Africa and Asia

**SA coffee market is consolidating fast.**
- BuiltforBusiness, Supply Chain Co., EURIPA, Taste of Africa all competing in B2B wholesale
- Key buyer pain points identified (from existing research):
  - **Inconsistent supply** — single-origin traceability is the #1 request
  - **Barista skill gap** — hotels/cafes want training bundled with bean supply
  - **Price opacity** — buyers don't know fair FOB vs landed cost
  - **Cold brew / seasonal** — summer demand for cold brew is unserved at B2B level

**HORECA (Hotels/Restaurants/Cafes) segment is underserviced by direct-to-roaster brands.**
- Top hotels (Saxon, Four Seasons, Table Bay, One&Only) still use multinational suppliers
- Independent specialty cafés want provenance stories — biltong+coffee gift bundles for VIP welcome packs are a real gap
- White-label bag opportunity: sell beans under Studex label to 10 SA cafés = recurring B2B revenue

### Opportunities for Studex
1. **Biltong + coffee gift bundle** — unique SA provenance story, no competitor doing it
2. **B2B coffee supply contract** — target Radisson Blu Gautrain, Michelangelo (conference/high-traffic)
3. **Cold brew wholesale** — summer is approaching SA, untapped B2B line
4. **China export via SA logistics** — SA port = cheaper logistics than Rwanda direct
