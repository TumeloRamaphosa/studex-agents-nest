## Topic 1: 🇿🇦 SOUTH AFRICA B2B COFFEE — HORECA LANDSCAPE

### Key Findings

**Market Structure**
- B2B channels account for ~60% of global coffee market — HORECA (Hotels, Restaurants, Cafés) is the core segment
- SA is a **net importer** of coffee → exposure to global price swings, FX risk, supply chain disruption
- Specialty Coffee market: $37.36B globally in 2026, growing at 6.86% CAGR to $52.06B by 2031
- SA at-home market still dominated by chicory/instant — the HORECA premium opportunity is real but needs education

**How Buyers Source**
- Hotels: Procurement via F&B suppliers, group purchasing orgs (EURIPA, TenderCube), or direct from roasters
- Offices: OCS providers (Dish Café, Vitago, Miko Africa, Corporate Coffee SA, Coffee Unplugged) bundle machine rental + bean supply — buyer rarely chooses beans directly
- Restaurants/Cafés: Independent specialty cafés buy direct from micro-roasters or specialty importers (Supply Chain Co., Trabocch)

**Critical Pain Points (what they complain about)**
- **Load shedding**: Power outages kill espresso machines mid-service; hotels need generator backup = added cost
- **Skilled barista retention**: Baristas undervalued, high turnover; training is a recurring cost nobody wants to absorb
- **Supply chain inconsistency**: Green coffee price volatility + rand weakness = menu pricing chaos
- **No brand story**: Generic bean suppliers offer no provenance narrative — nothing to put on the menu
- **Sustainability compliance**: Growing pressure from international hotel guests for EUDR-aligned, traceable sourcing

**Pricing Benchmarks**
- OCS bean subscription: ~R800–1,500/month per office (50-100 employees)
- Hotels (premium tier): R1,200–2,500/kg for specialty-grade green beans
- Restaurants: Prepared to pay R1,500–3,000/kg for single-origin if story is compelling

**Competitive Landscape**
| Player | Model | Gap Studex Fills |
|--------|-------|-----------------|
| Miko Africa | Machine + bean bundle | No training, no provenance story |
| Dallmayr | Premium brand, equipment | Expensive, not SA-owned story |
| Dish Café | OCS rental model | Commoditised, no specialty tier |
| Coffee Unplugged | Office specialist | No barista training |
| Origin Coffee (CPT) | SA micro-roaster | Competitor, but open to wholesale |

### Opportunities for Studex
1. **Barista training as wedge**: Nobody offers training + beans together. Bundle training into bean supply contracts → lock out Miko/Dish
2. **Provenance story**: Rwanda single-origin + African luxury positioning = exactly what 5-star hotels want on their menu
3. **Direct trade narrative**: SA hotels buying from Rwandan cooperative = brand story they can print on menus (ethical, traceable, African)
4. **Cold brew supply**: Summer pain point — no OCS player offers cold brew. High margin, growing demand
5. **Biltong + Coffee bundle**: Unique to Studex. VIP welcome packs, corporate gifting — zero competitors doing this

## Topic 2: 🔊 VOICEBOX — TUMELO VOICE CLONE ON ORGO VM

### Key Findings

**What Voicebox Is**
- Open-source, local-first AI voice studio (jamiepine/voicebox on GitHub)
- Free alternative to ElevenLabs and WisprFlow — runs entirely on your own hardware
- Voice cloning: just **3 seconds** of reference audio
- **7 TTS engines** built in (Qwen3-TTS, Chatterbox Multilingual, etc.)
- **23 languages** supported including Chinese and Arabic — key for Studex customer service
- MCP (Model Context Protocol) and ACP/A2A endpoints → plugs directly into OpenClaw
- REST API mode: turn into a local API server with zero rate limits, zero cloud costs

**Deployment Options**
| Option | Platform | Feasibility for Studex |
|--------|----------|------------------------|
| macOS app | Local Mac | ❌ Tumelo in Rwanda |
| Windows/Linux app | Desktop | ❌ Needs physical access |
| **Self-host server mode** | Orgo VM (Ubuntu) | ✅ **Best path — Charlie deploys tonight** |
| Cloud (voicebox.sh) | Web | ❌ Data privacy concern for WhatsApp |

**Self-Host on Orgo VM (Ubuntu)**
- Orgo VM: Ubuntu, 2 vCPU, 8GB RAM, 30GB disk — can run Voicebox server mode
- Voicebox server mode: one command, exposes REST API locally on VM
- GPU inference: Qwen3-TTS runs on CPU (slower but works; 8GB RAM is enough for light use)
