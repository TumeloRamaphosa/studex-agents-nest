## TOPIC 1 — COFFEE EXPORT: CHINA IS A DEFENSIVE MARKET, NOT AN EXPANSION ONE

### Key findings

**1. The market's value is contracting hard; the volume probably is not.**
- China imported **USD 96.4M** of coffee (HS 0901) in **August 2026**, down **USD 59.4M / −38.1%** from USD 156M. Exports from China were USD 11.7M — the deficit widened to USD 84.8M. *(Source: OEC bilateral coffee reporter, retrieved 26 Oct 2026.)*
- Arabica C was already **−23% YoY** at $3.06/lb (24 Oct, our own desk). If price is down ~23% and value is down ~38%, **volume is up roughly 10–20%**. The Chinese market is *drinking more and spending less* — a volume market with collapsing unit values.
- **Commercial consequence:** a buyer who buys on price is getting a commodity at a discount. A buyer who buys on **cup, traceability and tariff status** is the only buyer we can win. Our product (single washing station, Nyamasheke, 1,500–2,000m, A1/A2, SCA scores) is a *story* product. Sell the story at a price that a falling market can still absorb — which means the **roasted-at-origin line ($8.50/kg vs $3.80/kg green, both duty-free)** is now the centre of the offer, not a nice-to-have. Roast is what lets a buyer sell a differentiated cup into a commodity-price market.

**2. Africa's growth inside China is real but is a share story, not a market story.**
- China bought **$246M of African coffee in 2025, up from $157M in 2024** (+57% value); African exports to China **+145.7% YoY Jan–May 2026**. Top origins: Ethiopia, Kenya, **Rwanda**, Uganda.
- Read against the −38.1% headline: Africa is **taking share inside a contracting market.** That is the defensible framing and it needs to be stated as such. Rwanda is already a top-4 African origin into China — our origin is not a hopeful unknown, it is a listed supplier. **Use that.** *(Barista Magazine, 28 Jul 2026 — Barista Magazine via web search, dated.)*

**3. 🔴 CIIE 2026 registration is closed. Three options remain, none of them "book a booth."**
- **Exhibitor:** the official exhibitor registration portal now serves **"JOIN 2027 CIIE"** — the 2026 exhibitor round has closed. *(ciie.org/exhibition/f/book/register, retrieved 26 Oct.)*
- **Visitor/buyer:** online registration closed **20 Oct 2026 24:00 Beijing**; payment deadline **23 Oct 2026**; **no on-site registration for overseas professional visitors.** *(bytesim CIIE visitor workflow guide, 13 Oct 2026 — a travel vendor, not CIIE itself. ⚠️ Treat as indicative; confirm with the CIIE Bureau before repeating it externally.)*
- **What is still open:** the 9th CIIE runs **5–10 Nov 2026**, National Exhibition and Convention Center, Shanghai. The booth itself is not a prerequisite for a meeting. **The meeting is the asset; the booth is the wrapper.**
- **Three live routes, ranked:**
  1. **Ask the CIIE Bureau directly for late entry.** Their own site says *"Contact CIIE staff directly."* One email, one phone call. Even a late-walk-in visitor pass is worth more than nothing. **Owner: Tumelo. Tonight or tomorrow morning.**
  2. **Buy a badge through a partner.** A Chinese importer/distributor already exhibiting (Yaoji Food, JD, FreshHema) can add our people to their party — common practice at CIIE, and it costs us a favour, not a fee. **Our buyer list already contains exactly the right counterparties.**
  3. **Do not attend as an exhibitor; attend as a seller with a buyer list.** We already have 8 named target buyers (Luckin, Tim Hortons China, Pacific Coffee, Yaoji, JD, FreshHema, Heytea, NIO Life). **We have never contacted any of them.** The CIIE badge is irrelevant if we walk in with nobody to meet.

**4. The zero-tariff structural advantage survives all of this — and it is now the *lead* argument, not the opening line.**
- Zero tariff on **100% of tariff lines** from all 53 African countries with diplomatic ties, effective **1 May 2026**. Coffee previously carried **8–15%**.
- **Rwanda is an LDC → no expiry.** South Africa, Kenya, Nigeria, Egypt (non-LDC) lose the treatment **30 April 2028**. Our lots sit in the permanent category. **Every competitor whose window closes in 2028 has to re-justify their position in 2028; we do not.** In a contracting market, that is the single most durable thing we can say.
- The number the buyer cares about: at $6.75/kg, an 8–15% duty is **$0.54–$1.01/kg** — **$32–$61 on a 60kg bag**, handed back to them. In a falling-value market, a duty rebate is worth more than a discount, because it does not touch our margin.
- 🔴 **Unchanged from 25 Oct and still unfixed in copy:** the Certificate of Origin must **explicitly cite China's zero-tariff preference**. A generic FTA-origin form = MFN applied at 8–30%, retroactively. This is the most expensive one-line omission in the business.

**5. EUDR status — no new delay. Our 30 Dec 2026 date stands.**
- Confirmed across multiple independent sources: **large/medium operators 30 Dec 2026, micro/small 30 Jun 2027** (Reg. 2025/2650). The European Commission has said it **will not reopen the date.** One blog referencing a "postponement" refers to the *same* 12-month postponement that produced 30 Dec 2026 — **not a newer slip.** *(EY, 20 Jan 2026; Mayer Brown, 18 Feb 2026; EC Access2Markets.)*

## TOPIC 2 — CAVIAR: A MARGIN PLAY IN A SLOW MARKET, WITH A FORMAT DECISION TO MAKE

### Key findings

**1. The caviar market is growing slowly and absolutely — do not build a volume plan on it.**
- Africa's caviar market is forecast to reach **USD 529M by 2035 on a 1.6% CAGR**, reaching **~2.5k tonnes** (IndexBox, Feb 2026). Global caviar: **USD 480M (2025) → USD 519M (2026) → USD 962M (2035)** (Fortune Business Insights).
- **Read:** Africa's 1.6% CAGR is a *premium/luxury* market growing below inflation. This is a **margin and positioning play, not a volume play.** Any plan that assumes unit growth in SA caviar consumption is wrong. Our case must be *margin per 100g* and *which hotel groups list us*, not tonnage.
- **SA is named as one of three meaningful markets in the Middle East & Africa block** (UAE, Saudi, South Africa) — cited for premium hospitality and luxury dining. Our market is correctly chosen.

**2. 🟡 Format is shifting to frozen — and this is a live decision in our trade desk.**
- Mordor Intelligence (11 Sep 2026): **frozen formats are gaining ground**, while **Beluga held 36.96% of caviar market share in 2025** on scarcity and price premium; Osetra was the leading product segment at **34.6% in 2024** (Credence Research).
- **Implication:** frozen caviar (sturgeon caviar, non-viable caviar) is a *different product with different customers* — hospitality kitchens and banquet operations price and spec differently from gifting and retail. If frozen is taking share, then our current fresh-oscietra-only positioning covers **less of the market than our landed-cost model assumes**, and our high fixed landed cost is being compared against a product that carries a *lower* cold-chain burden.
- ⚠️ **This is a scope note for the trade desk, not a product change tonight.** But it is the first time a market source has pointed at a format our sheet does not cover. **Add a frozen-format line to `caviar-landed-cost.xlsx` and model it** — it may be the answer to our landed-cost problem, not a new problem.

**3. Species-name discipline is now a legal requirement, not a style preference.** *(Carried from 25 Oct, unchanged, still not in the copy.)*
- The EU has banned Russian caviar imports since April 2022; the US has enacted **up to 100% tariff authority on goods from top importers of Russian goods** (law signed Sept 2026).
- **Oscietra, Sevtruga, Kaluga are species descriptors — fine.** **"Russian" as a source claim is now a liability.** Our defensible position is **farmed Chinese sturgeon with CITES documentation and full traceability** (96% of global caviar consumption is farm-raised; China is the largest farmed producer). **Check every surface — trade page, spec sheet, invoices — for any "Russian" provenance claim. Our caviar trade page is live and publicly indexed.**
- CITES export permit + the registered exporter code on each tin is a legal requirement, not paperwork. Any caviar without CITES documentation is a seizure.

**4. The SA permit stack is still unassembled.** *(Carried from 25 Oct — DFFE + ITAC frozen-fish notification + SARS customs + CITES. Nothing has moved. This remains the gating item for any SA caviar sale, and it is a Tumelo action, not an agent action.)*

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Stop modelling caviar as a volume business.** 1.6% CAGR in Africa. The prize is (a) a defensible landed cost, (b) 3–5 anchor hotel groups listing us, (c) margin per tin. **Price and channel, not tonnage.**
2. **Model the frozen format.** If frozen is gaining share and carries a lower cold-chain burden, our landed-cost problem may be solvable *inside* the existing product set rather than by cutting price. **Highest-leverage caviar task this month.**
3. **Lead with farmed + CITES + traceable.** In a market where "Russian" is now radioactive, traceability is a *sales asset*, not a compliance chore. Put the CITES code and farm origin on the spec sheet and on the trade page.
4. **Position against Beluga (scarcity) not with it.** We cannot win a scarcity war. Beluga holds 36.96% on scarcity and price premium; **we compete on consistency, supply security and price-per-100g for a menu that needs to serve every night** — Osetra-family at a food-service-accessible price.

### Recommended actions

- [ ] **Add a frozen-format variant to the caviar landed-cost model** and compute it against the fresh-oscietra line. One SKU, one evening. **This is the Build Hour candidate.**
- [ ] **Audit every live caviar surface for "Russian"/provenance claims** — trade page, spec sheet, invoice template, the trade-desk tool. Live and indexed = high consequence, low effort.
- [ ] **Add the CITES exporter code + farm-origin line to the spec sheet** as a visible differentiator.
- [ ] **Tumelo action: assemble the SA import permit stack** (DFFE + ITAC + SARS + CITES). Still the gate on the entire SA caviar line. Unchanged since 25 Oct.
