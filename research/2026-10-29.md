## 🔴 LEAD FINDING OF THE NIGHT

**We are planning a caviar business on a 30% import duty and a 48-tonne Russian supply base, while South Africa is the one country in Africa that already imports caviar — and we are not the ones importing it.**

Three things fell out of tonight's caviar work, and they point in one direction:

1. **HS 1604.31 (prepared/preserved caviar & substitutes) carries 30% import duty + 15% VAT in South Africa** (SARS 2026 schedule, via JLog). Our landed-cost model was built on the permit stack; **the 30% duty line is the number that moves the R12,500/R22,000/R34,000 trade prices.** I did not open `caviar-landed-cost.xlsx` tonight — the exec loop guard cut my last read — so I am reporting the duty as *found*, not as *modelled*. It must be put through the model before any price is quoted.
2. **Russian caviar is a rounding error, not a supply line.** Russian farmed sturgeon caviar output was **45 t (Jan–Jul 2026), +5% y/y; 48 t (Jan–Aug 2026); 89 t for full-year 2025** (Shev, Tridge). Meanwhile **China shipped USD 118.2m = 42.1% of the global black-caviar trade and produced an estimated 260 t of farmed caviar** (Dataintelo, Mordor). Chinese large-scale sturgeon farming is what has **lowered global wholesale prices.** Positioning our product as "Russian caviar" imports us into the thinnest supply pool on earth and away from the one that actually sets the price.
3. **The trade flow runs the other way from our ambition.** Africa's caviar data: **imports 18 t ($935k) led by South Africa; exports 17 t ($4.6m) dominated by Africa-origin producers.** South Africa is Africa's **importing** node. Our 2027 proposition is currently "we will import caviar into the country that already imports more of it than anyone else in Africa" — which is a *legitimate and strong* position (we know the corridor), but it must be an **import-and-distribute** business, not a "we make caviar in Africa" story.

**The cheap fix is tonight; the expensive one is the build.** The live caviar trade page `ess0ocgcd9f0` advertises live retail + trade prices (R4,200 / R2,800 / R1,600 per 100 g and R34,000 / R22,000 / R12,500 trade) for a product whose permit stack does not exist. **Price advertising is the exposure; the catalogue is the pipeline.** Strip the prices, keep the catalogue, add one line — *supply subject to import permit*. That preserves the 2027 pipeline and removes the thing a competitor or a regulator can actually point at.

## TOPIC 1 — SA B2B COFFEE: THE CHANNEL WE HAVE A PAGE FOR AND HAVE NEVER SOLD INTO

### Key findings

**1. 🔴 The industry serving SA hospitality is telling us exactly why we are not being bought — and it is not price.** Hostex's 2026 sector brief names the constraints SA cafés, restaurants and hotels operate under: **(a)** SA imports most of its coffee, so it is *"particularly vulnerable to global price and supply fluctuations"*; **(b)** *"rising operational costs and ongoing load shedding"*; **(c)** **training and retaining skilled baristas is a major issue — the profession is often undervalued**, even though skilled baristas are what makes the specialty experience; **(d)** guests now expect single-origin, dialed espresso and alt-brew *in hotels, restaurants and conference venues*, not just cafés. **Every one of those four is a product we can sell.** We have the origin story (a), the low-cost-supply story (b), and we have the one thing nobody else offers (c) — training.

**2. 🟢 Our own file already identified the white space 7 weeks ago, and we have not built it.** `sa-b2b-coffee-prospects.md` (9 Aug) says it plainly: the SA office-coffee-service players — **Dish Café, Vitago, Coffee Devices SA, Caffè Latte** — are *"competitors AND potential partners"* and that **"none of these OCS players offer barista training or cold brew. Adding training converts bean supply into a retainer contract."** Seven weeks later the folder still holds a prospects list and an outreach template. **We have a list of 25 named buyers and no offer to send them.**

**3. 🟡 The market is real and the buyer is margin-squeezed — so the offer must protect *their* margin, not ours.** SA hospitality market **USD 11.49bn (2025) → 12.19bn (2026) → 16.34bn (2031+)** (Mordor / ResearchAndMarkets). SA hospitality suppliers sit under Fedhasa's warning (Aug 2026) that *"South Africa's restaurant industry cannot continue to carry more costs"* — **food and beverage income fell 0.3% in real terms in May 2026 vs May 2025** (2019 prices). Meanwhile SA's coffee consumer base is large and young-skewing: **~6m SA coffee consumers, two-thirds of adults drinking coffee daily, instant still 76% of drinkers** (PDG, Mar 2026) — a *specialty* conversion market, not a volume market. **The sales argument is "higher margin per cup, stable price per kg, and a barista your staff can keep."** Not "our coffee is better."

**4. 🔴 We have no price sheet for a B2B buyer to approve.** The live SA hospitality page (`i09hd71cvt06`, source `sa-hospitality-coffee/index.html`) carries the story but **not the commercial terms.** Visible SA wholesale benchmarks: **Imvelo Brands 2026 wholesale list K450/kg ex-VAT** (via PDG, Apr 2026) · **BeanCo** bulk wholesale, roasted-to-order, nationwide, pricing that scales with volume, free delivery over R850 · **CoffeeDesk** claims to be SA's largest wholesale distributor of coffee, tea and accessories · **Kwanu Beans / North & Co** premium wholesale beans to restaurants, cafés, hotels, guest houses and offices · retail reference points **R245/1kg** (Bariz Italian) and **R195** (African Roasters Jabulani). **A procurement manager cannot approve a supplier who has not published a tiered wholesale sheet.** This is the single biggest blocker on this channel and it is fixable tonight without a single API key.

**5. 🟡 The channel competitors are bigger than us and none of them is a coffee-origin company.** SA's workplace-food incumbents are **Canteen** (national: vending, markets, coffee, dining — *"elevate your breakroom"*) and **CoffeeDesk** (B2B platform). Office Coffee Service globally is **USD 9.8bn (2025) → 20.59bn (2034), 8.6% CAGR**, with **office coffee machines USD 4.99bn (2026) → 6.65bn (2031)** and **MEA named an emerging market.** They sell *the machine and the consumable*. **They do not sell the farm, the origin, the story, or the trained barista.** Our whole premium is the thing they structurally cannot supply.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Training is the wedge, beans are the entry.** Training converts a transactional bean sale into a monthly retainer with our brand behind the cup. It costs us a trainer, not inventory — and it is the one thing the OCS majors do not do. **We can build this without Shopify, without a token, without stock.**
2. **Direct-trade with African origins is explicitly what the sector wants.** Hostex names *"partnering with local micro-roasters and establishing direct-trade relationships"* and *"building value chains with African coffee-producing countries (Ethiopia, Rwanda, Kenya)"* as the 2026 opportunity. Our Rwanda A1/A2 and origin story is a **named item on an industry trend list.** Say it in their language.
3. **Hotels are a distinct, higher-value door than cafés.** Hotels add **signature coffee programmes and in-room specialty coffee** — a hospitality-room, high-margin, brand-visible product we can supply as a **white-label sachet**. Our own prospects file already names the angle: *"bean supply + in-room coffee sachets for welcome packs + barista training for F&B staff,"* competing against EURIPA and Taste of Africa. **Nobody in our list is currently serving in-room.**
4. **Enterprise gifting is the crossover.** A hotel that takes our coffee also takes our biltong — and our biltong gift ladder (R295–R720 hampers) is *already built* and *already cheaper than our own corporate-gifting page*. **The same buyer buys both. One account, two products.**

## TOPIC 2 — CAVIAR: REGULATIONS, DUTY, DEMAND, COMPETITORS

### Key findings

**1. 🔴 The permit stack we already know is right, and the duty number we may have missed.** South Africa is a CITES party; **DFFE is the designated CITES management authority and CITES permits are issued by Marine and Coastal Management**; DFFE requires an import/export permit for **marine aquaculture product**. Layered on our existing stack (DFFE → ITAC frozen-fish notification → DAFF importer registration → SARS importer code → NRCS → CITES export permit + registered exporter code) is the money line: **HS 1604.31, prepared or preserved caviar and caviar substitutes = 30% import duty + 15% VAT (SARS 2026).** Note the classification hinge: **0305.20 is live/chilled/frozen; 1604.31 is the prepared/preserved (tinned) line that retail caviar actually lands on.** 30% is the highest of any line Studex imports. ⚠️ *Duty is quoted as found from a tariff-lookup service, not from the SARS schedule PDF itself, and not broker-confirmed. Treat as an input to be verified, not a settled figure.*

**2. 🔴 "Russian caviar" is a marketing position attached to a 48-tonne pool.** Russian farmed sturgeon caviar: **45 t Jan–Jul 2026 (+5% y/y), 48 t Jan–Aug 2026, 89 t for 2025 (+9%)** (Shev / Tridge). Compare **China: USD 118.2m = 42.1% of global black-caviar trade, ~260 t of farmed production** (Dataintelo, Mordor). Chinese scale has **lowered global wholesale prices** (Business Insider). Russia→China seafood export revenue is **+54% in the first 9 months of 2026** (TASS) — i.e. **Russian caviar is being sold into China, not waiting for us.** Additionally, the US passed the **Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026** (Senate, 8 Aug 2026) and Russia lost PNTR (US effective tariff on Russian goods 7.3%, Aug 2026). **South Africa does not sanction Russia — but our export customers in the EU and US will read a Russian-supply chain as a question, and the Global South buyer will read it as an exoticism, not a quality claim.**

**3. 🟢 Demand is real and growing, and it is not a Russian-caviar market.** Global caviar: **USD 446.9m (2026) → 664.33m (2031), 8.25% CAGR** (Mordor); **USD 522.6m (2026) → 1,003.7m (2034), 8.5% CAGR** (Straits); **MEA USD 45.11m (2025) → 89.05m by 2034**. Growth drivers named across all of them: **aquaculture/farmed supply, fine dining, luxury hotels, premium retail, high consumer purchasing power.** 💡 **Farmed Chinese caviar at scale is why wholesale is soft — which cuts both ways: our *trade* buyer gets a cheaper 100 g, but our *retail* buyer gets a product that is no longer scarce.** Our retail premium has to be earned by provenance, CITES traceability, tasting and pairing — not by the word Beluga.

**4. 🟡 South Africa is the import node of Africa — so the corridor is the asset.** Africa's caviar trade: **imports 18 t ($935k) led by South Africa; exports 17 t ($4.6m) from African producers.** Acipenser (Western Cape, since 2009, French-founded) made SA Africa's first caviar producer and won Michelin coverage. ⚠️ *These Africa figures are an indexbox aggregation of a broad caviar-and-sturgeon trade category and the $ per tonne do not reconcile with premium caviar pricing — **do not quote them as caviar price benchmarks.** The directional fact (SA imports) is the usable part; the unit economics are not.*

**5. 🔴 Our advertised prices sit above every mainstream international benchmark — and we cannot yet defend them.** Our live page advertises **Oscietra R4,200 / Kaluga R2,800 / Sevtruga R1,600 per 100 g** retail and **R34,000 / R22,000 / R12,500** trade. International references: **Oscietra ~£75–80/100 g** (Lemberg UK) · **ARS Italica Oscietra 100 g US$465** (Simon Johnson) · **Golden Osetra US$6,701/kg** (California Caviar) · **Beluga US$7,000–22,000/kg; Beluga Hybrid US$8–12/gram at premium suppliers.** Our Oscietra at **R42,000/kg** sits above UK specialist retail and below US premium — defensible **only** if the CITES paperwork, the origin story and the duty are all real, because a buyer can compare in one browser tab. **Today the paperwork is not real, so the comparison is a loss.**

**6. 🔴 Two compliance facts that are cheap to fix and expensive to ignore.** (a) **Every caviar import — wild or farmed — requires a valid CITES permit, and every tin must carry a non-reusable label bearing the species code.** (b) Our live storefront catalogue is **30 products, 0 caviar → caviar is not purchasable on studexmeat.com**, but the trade page `ess0ocgcd9f0` is public with live prices and a WhatsApp/mailto funnel only. **We are advertising a product we cannot sell, at prices we cannot yet defend, through a page anyone can screenshot.**

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Own the import-and-distribute position for SA.** SA already imports more caviar than any other African country and our permit stack (unlike a newcomer's) starts from the DFFE/DAFF axis we already work on for frozen fish. **The corridor is the moat, not the origin.**
2. **Farmed (Chinese) caviar fixes the cost base; wild/traceable fixes the price.** A **farmed Oscietra/Sevruga core range at an honest landed cost** plus a **small, scarce, CITES-traceable premium tier** is a better business than a catalogue of "Russian" names at aspirational prices. It also matches the growth driver every market report names.
3. **The trade page is a pipeline, and a pipeline does not need prices.** Strip prices → keep catalogue, grade, indicative tier, and a **"supply subject to import permit — trade accounts only"** line. Pipeline preserved, exposure removed, and it costs one edit.
4. **Binding hospitality: caviar is a hospitality product, and we have a hospitality door.** Fine dining and luxury hotels are the two demand drivers named in every caviar market report. **We already have a hospitality coffee page and a prospect list. Sell caviar to the same accounts in 2027 — with coffee in 2026.**

### Recommended actions

- [ ] **Re-run `caviar-landed-cost.xlsx` with (a) HS 1604.31 at 30% + 15% VAT, (b) current FX, (c) farmed-China pricing as the base case and Russian/other origin as a premium case.** Produce a **landed cost + a defensible retail band per grade.** Nothing else about caviar can be decided before this.
- [ ] **Strip the prices from the live caviar trade page and add the permit line.** One edit, removes the screenshot-able exposure, keeps the pipeline. *(Build Hour item 3.)*
- [ ] **Do NOT ship the caviar tiers in `studex-corporate-gifting/src/App.tsx`.** Gold R7,000 / Platinum R15,000 with "50g Oscietra (CITES-certified)" and "100g Imperial Beluga" remain **unbuilt, unapproved, un-permitted.** Keep the banner. **A "CITES-certified" claim on a page we ship is a false claim, and that is a different category of risk from an expensive box.**
- [ ] **Get broker confirmation on the HS line and duty.** One email to a SA food-fish clearing agent. 30% vs 0% decides whether the trade price is R12,500 or R18,000.
- [ ] **Say "Russian" only if the supply is genuinely Russian.** If we buy farmed Chinese Oscietra, we say so. Overclaiming origin on a CITES-listed product is the fastest way to lose the hospitality buyer who knows what Oscietra actually tastes like.
