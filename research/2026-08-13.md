## Topic 1: China-Africa Coffee — The Window Is NOW, Not Later

### Key Findings

- **China coffee market: $51.9B (2025) → $94.9B by 2031, growing at 10.6% CAGR.** Chinese consumption is growing at **30% annually.** This is not a niche — it's the world's most important coffee growth market.
- **Coffee from all 53 African countries: approved effective July 20, 2026.** Single continent-wide phytosanitary framework ("Green Channel") replaces bilateral-by-bilateral approval. Burundi and Ethiopia already approved. Rwanda, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania are top current suppliers.
- **African coffee exports to China surged 145.7% year-on-year in first 5 months of 2026** — and that's BEFORE most exporters adjusted their strategies. The surge is already happening.
- **Chinese consumers want specialty East African profiles** — fruit-flavored Arabica, floral notes, bold Robusta. Rwanda's profile is perfectly positioned. Ethiopian beans dominate current Chinese buyer interest.
- **Critical margin insight:** Green bean export = $3.80/kg. Roasted coffee export = $8.50/kg. That's a **124% margin uplift** for the same raw material. Whoever roasts locally captures the premium — not Chinese importers.

### What Chinese Buyers Require
- Phytosanitary certificates per GAC Announcement No. 68 of 2026
- Certificate of Origin (referencing zero-tariff framework)
- HS codes (10-digit)
- CIQ registration + Halal cert for animal products
- B2B channels: Alibaba.com with Trade Assurance is the dominant platform Chinese buyers use

### What Rwanda Women Cooperatives Need (to access this market)
1. Phytosanitary approval from their own government → China GAC
2. Chinese import license holder on the buyer side
3. Roasting capability OR a trade partner who can arrange it
4. First-mover positioning before Chinese buyers lock in long-term contracts

### Where Studex Fits
- **We are NOT a coffee exporter.** We are a trade bridge.
- **Scenario A:** Find a Rwandan women coop with approved export access → broker the sale to a Chinese buyer → take a margin on the spread. No capital needed.
- **Scenario B:** Find a Chinese trading company that wants African origin coffee → become their African sourcing agent → commission per container.
- **Scenario C:** SA-China trade corridor — SA is listed as a non-LDC state in the zero-tariff framework. Investigate if SA-processed (roasted) Rwandan-origin coffee qualifies for zero tariff into China.
- **Key differentiator:** No one is pitching "Rwanda women farmers → China specialty consumer" as a story. That's a PR + commercial opportunity simultaneously.

### ⚠️ Red Flags
- Rwanda only has modest coffee export volume vs Ethiopia/Kenya — Chinese buyers may not know Rwandan coffee yet. This is both a risk AND an opportunity.
- Diplomatic relations required — Rwanda has them. ✅
- Processing/Halal certification infrastructure takes time — but Q3 2026 is still early in this policy cycle.

## Topic 2: SA Luxury Meat E-Commerce — Competitive Landscape & Opportunities

### Key Findings

- **SA processed meat market: $242B (2026) → $3.38B by 2031, CAGR 6.68%.** Food e-commerce specifically is growing 30-35% YoY. Online premium meat is still early.
- **Market structure:** 65%+ of packaged meat sold through supermarkets. But premium/direct-to-consumer is the growth edge. Cold chain is the competitive moat — most local butchers can't deliver nationally.
- **Current Studex pricing from live site:** Wagyu biltong from R650, Gold 7kg box (100x70g packets) at R12,500. That's premium positioning. Where are competitors?
- **Competitor landscape:**

  - **Country Meat** (countrymeat.co.za) — mid-premium, multi-location, online store
