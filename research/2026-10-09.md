## Topic 1: Luxury Meat E-Commerce — Competitive Landscape

### Key Findings

- **SA e-commerce hit R159bn in 2026 (+22.5% YoY).** First year where online = 10% of all SA retail. The infrastructure tailwind is here — more South Africans buying premium goods online than ever.
- **Premium biltong pricing benchmarks:**
  - Studex Wagyu Biltong: **R650–R812.50/kg** (current positioning — healthy premium)
  - Wagyu Biltong Co (Australia): ~**$180 AUD/kg** (~R1,950/kg at current rates — AUD is weak, Studex is MORE competitive for SA buyers)
  - BiltongBoytjies Wagyu Drywors: **R930/kg** (bulk discount available, antibiotics-free claim)
  - L.A. Farms Angus Biltong: **R93–R110/unit** (smaller format, butchershop model)
  - Budget/premium mass market: R295–R500/kg (lower quality, no wagyu)
- **Studex is competitively priced at the premium end.** The Australia comparison actually works in our favour — Studex is significantly cheaper for SA buyers when import costs + shipping are factored.
- **Competitors differentiating on:** Story (L.A. Farms = Cape Town butcher heritage), certifications (antibiotics-free, free-range), subscription models (BiltongBoytjies free 1-4 day express over R1,000).
- **Gap in the market:** Nobody in SA luxury meat is doing **bundled luxury gifting** (biltong + premium coffee + leather gift box). High-margin, zero local competition.

### Opportunities for Studex

- **Reaffirm premium pricing.** We're not the most expensive — we're mid-premium with clear quality story. The Australia comparison is a sales asset, not a liability.
- **Add subscription option** for biltong (monthly 1kg wagyu delivery) — BiltongBoytjies is doing this and it works. Recurring revenue = business valuation.
- **Launch a gift bundle tier** — "The Studex Luxe Box": Wagyu Biltong + African single-origin coffee + branded torch ключ + handwritten card. Target: corporate gifts, hotel welcome packs, expat South Africans overseas.

## Topic 2: SA B2B Coffee — Market Deep Dive

### Key Findings

- **SA coffee market growing at 7.35% CAGR**, projected to cross **USD 1.1bn by 2030**. The market is accelerating, not slowing.
- **SA coffee bean market specifically: USD 0.65bn (2025) at 4.79% CAGR through 2034.** Beans = the B2B opportunity (not RTD or café sales).
- **Arabica prices down ~15% in 2026.** Rare window — green bean costs are falling while specialty demand is rising. NOW is the time to lock in supplier contracts before prices normalize.
- **Specialty coffee driving hotel F&B differentiation.** LinkedIn article (Hostex SA): "role of specialty coffee within hotels, restaurants, and cafés will only grow in significance." Key buyer criteria: provenance story, Rainforest Alliance/sustainability cert, barista quality.
- **Nespresso moving into SA small offices** (as noted in last session — confirmed 3 weeks ago). The SMB OCS market is being seeded by a giant. Studex should target the TOP end (5-star hotels) where Nespresso doesn't play, and use that beachhead to build credibility.
- **Fragmented SA coffee supply chain** — no dominant local B2B coffee brand. This mirrors the biltong market when Studex entered. Pattern recognition: Studex can be the dominant premium B2B coffee + meat supplier to SA luxury hospitality.

### Opportunities for Studex

- **Lock in green bean contracts NOW** while arabica is down 15%. Ethiopian/Rwandan single-origin, 250g/500g/1kg white-label bags for hotel F&B. Higher margin when bean costs are low.
- **Coffee-beans shortlist for white-label:** Ethiopian Yirgacheffe (fruity, floral), Rwandan Bourbon (chocolatey, smooth), Ugandan Robusta (for espressos — higher caffeine, cheaper). All African, all story-rich.
- **First mover advantage in bundling.** No SA supplier is selling "premium biltong + specialty coffee" as a unified hospitality offering. This is Studex's to lose.
- **Wolf Pack B2B angle:** Restaurants/cafes already in Wolf Pack get preferential coffee pricing. Single workflow: join Wolf Pack → get Studex meat listing + coffee supply access.
