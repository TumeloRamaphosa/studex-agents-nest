## TOPIC 1: 🌏 African Coffee — Who Is Buying in UAE, China, Europe?

### Key Findings
- **China pays PREMIUM prices for African coffee.** Kenyan data: China averaged $646/50kg bag vs global commodity avg ~$350. Rwanda and Ethiopia are in China's 53-country Africa coffee import protocol (opened 2026). This validates our China-first strategy.
- **China opened market to 53 African countries' coffee imports (Aug 2026).** First-mover window is NOW. SA-China bilateral agricultural protocol gives us legal access; Rwanda A1/A2 grades are precisely what Chinese specialty roasters want.
- **UAE/MENA coffee market: $17.1B in 2026 → $24.9B by 2031** (7.75% CAGR). Large, growing, underserved by African specialty originators. Hotel groups, specialty roasters, and restaurants are the buyer types.
- **EU specialty buyers bypass commodity price entirely** — paying €12–18/kg FOB for single-origin African (Ethiopia Yirgacheffe, Kenya AA, Rwanda). Direct trade relationships replacing commodity channels.
- **China top buyer by price, not volume.** Luckin Coffee and Tim Hortons China are volume buyers; the premium chains (Heytea, % Arabica China) pay specialty prices. Priority targeting confirmed.

### Opportunities for Studex
- Lock in 2–3 Chinese buyers at FOB $4.80–5.50/kg before the market floods with new entrants in 2027
- UAE strategy: target hotel groups directly (Emirates, Jumeirah) with Rwanda + biltong pairing pitch
- Build a "Founder's Reserve" limited micro-lot (500kg max) priced at €15–18/kg for EU specialty direct-trade — high margin, brand-building

## TOPIC 2: 🇿🇦 SA B2B Coffee — How Hotels & Restaurants Buy

### Key Findings
- **Specialty coffee is now the baseline expectation** in SA luxury hospitality — not a differentiator, a ticket to play. The Table Bay, One&Only, Saxon are already doing this.
- **Supply chain pain points:** 60–90 day lead times for green beans, freshness risk on roasted coffee (2–3 week window), no local Rwandan supplier. Gap = our opportunity.
- **Direct-trade + brand story > commodity bean supply.** Hotels want to put a farm, a farmer, a region on the menu. We have Rwanda origin — use it.
- **Barista training = retainer lock-in.** Currently no SA bean supplier bundles training. If we add this, we become a vendor, not just a product.
- **White-label opportunity:** Most SA hotels have mediocre in-house roasted coffee. Supply our beans with their brand label = recurring revenue, no marketing cost for us.

### Opportunities for Studex
- Target the 10-hotel list in `sa-b2b-coffee-prospects.md` with a **"Farm to Floor" sample kit** — 1kg Rwanda A1 + biltong tasting selection + origin story card
- Add barista training as a value-add in every B2B proposal (use it to justify premium pricing)
- Approach Dish Café and Vitago as white-label bean suppliers — they have the client relationships, we have the product
