## TOPIC 1 — Caviar into South Africa: regulatory + margin reality check

### Key findings

- **Compliance stack is unchanged and still the gate:** CITES (Appendix II, all sturgeon — wild *and* farmed) + DAFF import permit + health cert from origin + cold chain −2°C to +2°C. Local file `caviar-landed-cost.xlsx` carries CITES at **USD 15/kg** as a standing assumption. Prior brief (2026-09-21) confirms **ITAC Rebate Item 460.19** (caviar for further processing) is worth investigating — a potential 20% duty relief that has never been actioned.
- **Our own landed-cost model, recomputed tonight (verifiable from `caviar-landed-cost.xlsx`):**
  | Product | Farm gate | Duty 20% | VAT 15% | Total USD/kg | Landed ZAR/kg @18.5 | Trade price | Margin | Retail | Margin |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Oscietra Imperial | $1,000 | $200 | $182 | $1,411.50 | **R26,363** | R34,000 | +29.0% | R42,000 (R4,200/100g) | +59.3% |
  | Kaluga Prestige | $700 | $140 | $128 | $997.50 | **R18,704** | R22,000 | +17.6% | R28,000 | +49.7% |
  | Sevtruga Classic | $400 | $80 | $74 | $583.50 | **R11,045** | R12,500 | +13.2% | R16,000 | +44.9% |
  - ⚠️ **Our trade prices are BELOW the minimum viable price for a 30% margin on all three SKUs** (Oscietra min viable R37,661 vs trade R34,000; Kaluga R26,720 vs R22,000; Sevtruga R15,778 vs R12,500). **We are currently selling trade caviar below our own floor.** This is a pricing bug, not a market problem.
- **Our retail pricing is at/above the indexed SA premium band.** IndexBox (SA Caviar Market Report, Sep 2026): premium caviar retail in SA typically **R8,000–R35,000/kg**. Our Oscietra retail of **R42,000/kg (R4,200/100g)** sits *above* the top of that band. Prior local research (2026-09-21) put SA retail at R2,000–6,000/100g for Osetra/Kaluga/Sevruga. **We are priced at the very top of, or outside, the observed market.** Either we are the premium-brand play (and must prove it), or we are overpriced for the trade buyer who can buy Kaluga at R2,000–3,000/100g.
- **Market context:** Middle East & Africa caviar market USD 45.11m (2025) → USD 89.05m (2034); African caviar market to USD 529m at 1.6% CAGR; SA is Africa's **import hub** (18 t, ~USD 935k) while **Madagascar (Rova) dominates African exports** (~17 t, USD 4.6m). SA is a net importer — structurally the right place to be a distributor.
- **Competitors:** **Aquatir** (SA's own sturgeon farm — Beluga, Russian sturgeon, Sterlet, Bester — direct farm-to-buyer, no import chain); **Rova (Madagascar)** ("Africa's first caviar", premium $10,000+/kg top grades); **Euro Meats (JHB)** and Cape Town fine-dining distributors supplying The Test Kitchen / Greenhouse / Table Bay; and 5-star hotels importing direct from European farms, bypassing local importers entirely. **No SA D2C caviar brand exists.** *(All from local research files 2026-09-21, 2026-10-11, 2026-08-14.)*
- **Sanctions reality:** EU, UK and US all ban Russian caviar imports; **SA has NOT imposed autonomous sanctions on Russian caviar**, but payment rails have tightened and Dubai/UAE re-export is the de facto corridor. Our landed-cost sheet is built on **Moscow → SA airfreight**, which is the assumption most exposed to this friction.

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Fix the trade price sheet before the next caviar quote goes out.** Three SKUs are below our own modelled floor. Raise trade price or renegotiate farm gate — a 29/18/13% trade margin cannot fund a CITES 4–8 week lead time plus cold-chain failure risk.
2. **Rebenchmark retail against Aquatir and the local R2,000–3,000/100g Kaluga/Sevruga band.** The D2C white space is real, but only with an entry SKU (Sevruga/Kaluga at ~R2,000–2,500/100g) to pull the brand in, with Oscietra as the halo.
3. **Chase ITAC Rebate 460.19** — a 20% duty relief on a product where duty is 14–17% of landed cost is worth ~R3,000–R4,000/kg on Oscietra. Nobody has pulled this thread.
4. **Use the Dubai/UAE corridor for both caviar AND coffee** — one relationship, two lanes, and it sidesteps the Moscow SWIFT problem. We already have UAE coffee activity from the China research.
5. **Sell the compliance, not the eggs.** Aquatir is farmed SA. Our edge is provenance (CITES-compliant Caspian import) + being the only party who bothered with the paperwork. That's a hospitality-buyer argument.

## TOPIC 2 — Luxury meat e-commerce: competitors & pricing benchmarks

### Key findings

- **Biltong is a large, growing, globalising category.** Global biltong market **USD 1.53bn (2025) → USD 3.03bn by 2034**; the snack segment alone USD 460m (2025) → USD 845.7m by 2034 at 7.0% CAGR, led by South Africa. *The Economist (23 Sep 2026): "Biltong is going global — like South Africans"* — new brands in Berlin, Brooklyn, Hong Kong and Singapore. **Export demand is real and we have no export story yet.**
- **Our retail biltong price is right in the pack, at the top of it.** Benchmarks found tonight:
  | Brand | Product | Price |
  |---|---|---|
  | Checkers | 100g sliced/drywors/snapsticks | R50–70 per 100g (cheapest, R500–700/kg) |
  | Woolworths | chilli beef biltong snapsticks | **R1,053/kg** |
  | Meat World / Matumi | 1kg Wagyu beef biltong | R699/kg |
  | Biltong Boytjies | typical wagyu biltong/drywors | R499.99–599.99/kg |
  | Famous Kalahari | Wagyu biltong 0.5kg | R492.50 (R985/kg) |
  | Bruchs Biltong | Wagyu 1kg | R799 |
  | **Studex (live store)** | **Luxury Wagyu Biltong 1kg** | **R650** (500g R450) |
  **Read:** R650/kg undercuts Bruchs (R799) and Kalahari (R985) but sits above the promo floor (Checkers R500–700/kg equivalent, Meat World R699). We are **not the cheapest** — so the argument must be provenance/quality, not price. Note the store's own **500g at R450 = R900/kg** vs **1kg at R650** — the per-kg price falls 28% at volume, which is good merchandising but worth a bundle anchor.
- **Processed-meat market is growing faster than our category.** SA processed meat market **USD 2.31bn (2026) → USD 3.18bn by 2031, 6.68% CAGR**; non-store retail (e-commerce) is the fastest-growing segment. Global meat-subscription market USD 2.2bn (2026) → 5.5bn by 2033 (13.9% CAGR).
- **The global subscription playbook is mature and we have no equivalent.** ButcherBox (US), Chop Box, Carnivore Club ($220–240/mo organ-fortified boxes), The Carnivore Society (A$300/mo, customer picks cuts, 5kg ~US$180). Winning pattern everywhere: **customer chooses cuts, box price is entry-priced at $50–150, "from a real butcher" trust claim.**
- **Global meat-delivery standards have moved:** ButcherBox and Good Chop ship in **1–3 days** with free delivery over threshold; free delivery over R1,000 is the SA norm (e.g. Mos Butchery). Our WhatsApp-order funnel has no visible delivery promise or threshold — that's a conversion leak.
- **Our own catalogue is stronger than our storefront.** `studex-products-catalog.csv` carries ~50 active SKUs with clean cost/price pairs (ribeye 1kg R1,950→R2,939; Wagyu Biltong Gold 7kg R12,500; caviar R2,600–4,200; coffee R270–400). **We are selling 2 SKUs of a 50-SKU catalogue.**

### Opportunities for Studex

1. **Launch a curated "Wagyu Box" subscription before the year-end gifting rush** — pick-your-cuts, monthly, from the catalogue we already own. This is the model every global competitor is running, and it converts a one-off R650 biltong order into recurring revenue.
2. **The biltong export story is unclaimed and it's now newsworthy** (Economist, Sept 2026). We already have CAF Noir branding and 6 green-coffee origins exported via the Wildlife Coffee page — **the same B2B export page could carry biltong**. Berlin/Brooklyn/Hong Kong importers are actively looking for SA biltong brands right now.
3. **Institutional + consumer + subscription, three price ladders off one catalogue** — we already have the institutional sell sheet live (R380–410/kg biltong ex-VAT, R1,050/kg ribeye) and the retail store live. The missing rung is the subscription.
4. **Free delivery over threshold + a stated next-day/48h promise** is the cheapest conversion fix available in the meat category right now.
5. **Wholesale MOQ pages already exist** (5kg biltong weekly, 10 bags coffee fortnightly). Nothing is wrong; nothing is being pushed.

### Recommended actions

- [ ] **Publish a price-positioning card** for the sales team: our biltong sits mid-pack, not cheapest — pitch provenance, halal certification, export-grade packaging, not price.
- [ ] **Design one subscription SKU, not a tiered programme.** Start with a single monthly "Wagyu & Biltong Box" at ~R1,200–1,500/mo with cut selection, matching the ButcherBox/Chop Box pattern. Use catalogue SKUs; no new production.
- [ ] **Add a delivery-threshold + delivery-promise line to every meat page and the WhatsApp order flow.**
- [ ] **Add a biltong line to the Wildlife Coffee export page** (it's already the B2B export asset) and start one EU importer conversation, timed to the Economist "biltong going global" wave.
