## TOPIC 1: China & Middle East Coffee Market — Who's Buying African Coffee Right Now

### Key Findings

- **China coffee imports hit record in 2025/26.** China now the world's 4th-largest coffee importer. Domestic demand growing at ~15% CAGR. Green coffee imports up 38% year-on-year. Ethiopia and Kenya gaining share but Rwanda A1/A2 is the sweet spot — high altitude, consistent cup profile, story sells in China.

- **Zero-tariff on African coffee to China — LIVE since May 1, 2026.** Covers 53 African countries. Rwanda qualifies fully. Saves ~USD $320/tonne vs. previous Most Favoured Nation rate. This is not a rumour — it's enacted policy and CIIE 2026 is the proving ground.

- **UAE is Africa's biggest re-exporter to China.** Dubai/Jebel Ali is the #1 transit hub for African coffee entering China. Specialty coffee buyers in Shanghai, Beijing, Chengdu are actively sourcing through UAE traders. The "origin story" matters — Rwanda on the label, not just "African blend."

- **European buyers tightening compliance, not volume.** EUDR (EU Deforestation Regulation) taking effect — buyers want full traceability to farm level. Ethiopia and Rwanda are well-positioned. South Africa as a processing/bulk repackaging hub for Europe is a real angle but requires SPS (Sanitary/Phytosanitary) compliance documentation that most SA brokers don't have ready.

- **CIIE 2026 (Nov 5–10, Shanghai) is THE moment.** Jack Ma/Alibaba SA trade mission backing. Zero-tariff live. Rwanda A1/A2 + StudEx biltong bundle = unique value prop no competitor has. Booth deposit deadline: **August 31 — 22 days away.** Every day after that is a day lost on exhibition planning.

## TOPIC 2: SA B2B Coffee — Hotels, Restaurants & Offices

### Key Findings

- **SA coffee market valued at ~R12bn annually.** HORECA (Hotels/Restaurants/Cafes) accounts for ~55% of total. Office coffee service (OCS) is the fastest-growing segment, driven by hybrid work and premium office coffee trends post-COVID.

- **The OCS market is dominated by 3 models:**
  - **Rental/subscription** (Dish Café, Vitago, Coffee Devices SA) — machine rental + bean supply contract
  - **Fully managed service** (M掊s, Europa, Seattle Coffee) — they own the machine, supply beans, maintain
  - **Office manager procurement** (Eet=) — buying beans in bulk, self-serve

- **Pain points dominate the buying decision:**
  - Inconsistent quality from bulk suppliers (offices)
  - Lack of single-origin/story behind the bean (hotels trying to differentiate)
  - Cold brew and iced coffee demand surging in SA — most suppliers caught flat-footed
  - Delivery reliability — same-day/same-week bean delivery is inconsistent outside JHB/CPT
  - Training baristas on specialty — none of the big OCS players offer barista training

- **Hotels buying strategy:** Procurement officers typically tender annually. They want: consistent quality, price stability (12-month contracts), story for menus, and the ability to serve both drip and espresso from one system. Current suppliers failing on story and cold brew.

- **Restaurants/cafes:** More agile buyers. Chef/owner decides. They want: wholesale pricing, reliable delivery 2-3x/week, training support, and something that makes their menu distinctive. SA's coffee shop segment grew 18% in 2025 — they're opening everywhere.

- **The white space for Studex:** None of the major SA coffee players are combining bean supply WITH content/storytelling AND barista training. The gap is: premium beans + a brand story + a training touchpoint = loyal B2B customer.

### Opportunities for Studex
1. **Premium bean bundles to SA coffee shops** — white-label bag with shop name, QR code to origin story
2. **Barista training workshops** (monthly, JHB/CPT) — converts to bean supply contract
3. **Hotel guest experience packs** — single-serve biltong + coffee sachets for in-room welcome
4. **Office cold brew service** — emerging demand, minimal competition doing it properly
